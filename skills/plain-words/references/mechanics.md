# The two passes over a draft

Read this when you have a draft and want the passes themselves, not the rule. The structural pass
makes a sentence hard to misread; the tells pass removes the writing that marks text as an agent's.
`skill://plain-words` holds the rule each pass serves: every verb names something that happened.

Run the structural pass first: a tell often disappears with the sentence that carried it.

## The structural pass

1. One instruction per sentence. A sentence that holds two actions hides the second from a reader
   who stops at the first.
2. Active voice, with the actor named. "The job is retried" leaves the reader to guess who retries
   it; "the daemon retries the job" does not.
3. Simple tenses. A compound tense carries a time relation the reader has to reconstruct, so keep
   one only where that relation is the fact.
4. Write the thing, not its noun form: "the build failed", not "a build failure occurred". A noun
   made from a verb drops the actor and the time.
5. Cut a noun stack to three words. Four or more leave the reader guessing which word modifies
   which.
6. One idea per paragraph, and a list wherever you write three or more of anything. A reader
   counts a list and cannot count a sentence.
7. Keep every qualifier, exception, number and hedge. Where keeping one makes the sentence long,
   keep it long and say why: dropping "may" turns a possibility into a claim.
8. Use one word for one thing, through the whole text. A synonym reads as a second thing.

## The tells pass

1. Delete a sentence that says what you are about to say, or what you have just said. A reader who
   has the point does not need it announced.
2. Delete praise of the work and of the reader: "great question", "a robust solution".
3. Delete the symmetry an agent reaches for: "not only X but also Y", "it is not just A, it is B".
   Write the half that is true.
4. Delete a hedge that costs a second read: "it may be worth considering whether". Recommend it or
   leave it out.
5. Delete a closing offer to do more. Where something remains, name it as the next step with its
   owner.
6. Cut an em dash and a semicolon into two sentences, unless the aside is the fact.
7. Replace a word that rates the thing ("comprehensive", "significant", "seamless") with the
   measurement that made you reach for it.
8. Read the first and last sentence of each paragraph alone. Where they say the same thing, delete
   one.

## Where a fuller pass exists

`skill://asd-ste100` is the full controlled-language standard behind the structural pass: run it
where a reader must not misread one sentence. Neither pass here replaces `skill://plain-words`,
which is the rule both serve. Where your session installs a fuller pass of its own over AI writing
tells, run that one after the tells pass above; this file is what you run when it does not.
