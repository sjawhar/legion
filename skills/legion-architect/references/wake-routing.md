## Wake routing

Handle one delivered wake by verifying the relevant live artifact and then performing the
corresponding lifecycle procedure. Architect-addressed wakes always reach the architect that owns
the payload issue: a claimed child sub-architect, otherwise the nearest claimed ancestor, then the
root. A wake about an architect role's own launch reaches the architect above it. `phase-finished`,
worker lifecycle, child lifecycle, and design-gate wakes are architect-only and never go to an
active phase worker.

The four merge-notice rows are one sentence each; read
`skill://legion-architect/references/merge-notices.md` for the notice that woke you — what its
`reason` names, what the daemon has stopped doing, and what you read before you ask a person.

A "section N" below is that section of `skill://legion-architect`. The spec and gate steps of
section 1 are in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`; the detail of sections 3, 6
and 7 (a conflict, the hand-over, the close) is in
`skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`.

| Wake | Procedure |
| --- | --- |
| `carried-gate-opened` | Only a root whose catch-up said `carried` gets it. Its `version` and `reason`: a human approved the old root's spec document at `version`, which opened the gate this root carried; the daemon has released any READY it refused and started any child waiting on it. It is not `design-approved`: do not proceed to section 2 or release a wave because of it, and start nothing. |
| `child-status` | A child of your tree left the workflow or re-entered it, or joined your tree from the admission queue; the notice's reason names the child and its new status, or reads `<KEY> joined the tree of <ROOT> from the admission queue`. `todo` (a human's move, your `release_children`, or your `rerun_child`) means the child runs again under your tree from planning, and the daemon starts it; you start nothing. `backlog`, `icebox` or `triage` (a human's move, or your `park_child`) means the daemon has suspended the child's workers, and it advances no further until it is set back to `todo`. Joined from the admission queue means a root that waited for a slot and never ran, which a person made a child of your tree or your `release_children` adopted, now runs in your tree with no slot or architect of its own, from planning when it is `todo` and parked otherwise; you start nothing. What the rest of the tree does is your decision. |
| `child-closed` | Read the child completion and remaining open children. Re-scope open work, park obsolete work with `park_child`, and release an appropriate next wave with `release_children`. When a worker waits on this child for a missing surface (see `phase-finished`), tell it to continue with `envoy_publish` to its role topic. The last child's close is not the end-game (section 3). |
| `child-release-stalled` | Its `reason` names a child your `release_children` released while Dispatch already showed it `todo`, and that child's parent, whose release has not reached the daemon after three tries. Read the parent's record with the `legion` tool's `read_record` (`issue` the parent); run `release_children` for the child again once the parent is recorded in your tree, and tell the controller if it never is. |
| `child-not-recorded` | Its `reason` names a child your tree did not record and why: its repository resolves to none (neither it nor an ancestor names one, and the project has no default), or to one not mapped to the project, or to one the daemon leaves out for a missing GitHub App. The daemon said why on the child too. Set the child's repository to one of the project's (`dispatch issue-update --repo owner/name`), then release it again with `release_children`; tell the controller when the repository it needs is not served. |
| `design-approved` | Its `version` is the approved one. A human approved the root spec document at its current version; the gate is open. Proceed to section 2 — except on a root whose catch-up said `handed_over`: its workers already run their phases, so start nothing; the approval releases any READY the gate refused. One whose `version` is the version a human's own answer to a decision block wrote, after an earlier approval (Dispatch carried that approval to it, "After approval" in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`), opens nothing new: carry on, and release no wave because of it. |
| `design-changes-requested` | Its `version` and `reason`. A human asked for changes to the root spec at `version`, for `reason`. Revise the spec as the reason asks and request approval again as "The armed gate" in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md` says; end your turn; the gate is closed. |
| `phase-finished` | The daemon has already moved the issue to its next phase by its fixed table and started that phase's role; you start nothing. Read the committed handoff for the finishing phase; if it shows unresolved gaps, tell the role now working the issue (`envoy_publish` to its role topic). A `planner` notice that names a departure from the spec's design defers to the full condition in "After approval" of `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`: when the approved Summary, Acceptance, scope and settled decisions still hold, the plan is the record; otherwise change the root spec and request approval again as that section says. A `reviewer` notice whose GitHub review is `CHANGES_REQUESTED` needs nothing from you: the daemon has returned the issue to `implementing` (Dispatch `in_progress`) and started the **implementer**, whose correction goes through the tester and the reviewer again, never straight to retro. A `reviewer` notice with an `APPROVED` review means the issue is in `accepting` and the acceptance is yours (section 6): read the evidence, then `sign_off` or send it back with `request_backward_move`. A `tester` notice with `verdict: "fail"` — its handoff carries `implementerProof.verdict: "rejected"`, or a failure naming the production-like proof — has gone back to the **implementer** by the daemon's table; never supply the proof from another role. A worker that reports no surface reaches the changed path sends that report instead of completing its phase, so the daemon starts nothing more on that issue and the worker stays idle in its session, not suspended: file a child issue in this tree to build the surface (infrastructure, tooling, or a skill), and when that child's `child-closed` arrives, tell the waiting worker to continue with `envoy_publish` to its role topic. That report is never a reason to advance the phase. |
| `pr-blocked` | Its `reason` names the pull request and the `max_fix_attempts` it reached, or reads `Issue reached review_round_cap=<N>.` (the end of this row). The count is of heads pushed onto a red verdict that changed something outside `.legion/` — handoff-only pushes (`.legion/` paths only) never count, a push by the review App (a planner's, tester's, reviewer's or architect's) never counts, and the head after a red the tester's red tests earned (a review-App push that changed a path outside `.legion/`, however many handoff-only pushes follow it) does not count either — so after the tester's handoff-only push onto the implementer's red, the implementer's next push does count; a push the daemon cannot classify (a listener without `changed_paths`, a list the listener stopped at 100 paths or 32,768 runes of text, a push listing no commits) does. Published once per exhausted count, not on every later red verdict for that count. Read the failed CI evidence and recovery attempts. The notice moves nothing, and no later CI red within the attempt that ran the count out (the counted head, and every head pushed on top of it until a push lands on a head CI judged green, the tester's red tests and handoff onto the held red included) sends the work back from `testing`, `reviewing`, `accepting`, `retro` or `merging` either: the issue stays in its phase until you move it, or a worker's own decision does (a tester's `fail`, a reviewer's request for changes). A head pushed onto green CI leaves that attempt, so its red still comes as a `checks-red`. In `implementing`, give the implementer the failing checks (`envoy_publish` to its role topic). In any later phase a worker holds, tell that worker (`envoy_publish` to its role topic) to move the issue back to `implementing` with `request_backward_move`, naming the failing checks, and the daemon starts the implementer; or file a corrective child. In `accepting`, where no worker holds a phase, the move is yours: `request_backward_move` with `issue`, naming the failing checks. In `awaiting_merge`, where no worker holds a phase and a backward move is refused, open a `dispatch ask` naming the failing checks for the human who merges. Do not treat the blocked PR as final. A `review_round_cap` reason is the implementer's round count instead: every move back to an earlier phase counts one, and the notice comes again with each round past the cap. It moves nothing either, since the round that reached the cap has already started its worker. Tell the rounds apart by what announced each: a tester's fail by its `phase-finished` with `verdict: "fail"`; a request for changes by the reviewer's `phase-finished` over a `CHANGES_REQUESTED` review; the daemon's own move by a `checks-red` notice, whose reason names a conflict (`the head conflicts with <base>`), the required checks that failed (`CI is red at <head>`), or the merge queue's failed merge and its failing run (`the merge queue took pull request #<n> out at READY's head …`); a worker's `request_backward_move` by a `phase-finished` from the role that asked, whose reason reaches only the task of the worker the move started, so ask that worker; your own send-back from `accepting` by the reason you gave. Judge the cap by the rounds that are not conflicts. Conflict returns need nothing from you beyond the next round. Tester fails, requests for changes and CI-red returns (a `checks-red` notice is raised for CI only when a required check fails on code the branch pushed, or when the merge queue fails the merge) are the rounds the cap is for. Each `review_round_cap` notice is a decision you make in the turn it wakes, never one left for the next round to make: read what every counted round found, choose one of the three below, act on it in the same turn, and post it as a `dispatch comment` on the issue naming the rounds you read and the choice. **Narrow**: the change itself is sound but each round finds more around it; tell the implementer and the reviewer (`envoy_publish` to each one's role topic) which acceptance lines the next round answers; a correctness finding is still fixed in this pull request, and only naming, duplication or wording cleanup moves to the `Fast-follow:` line. **Split**: part of the work cannot settle in this pull request. On a child, file that part as a child of this issue's parent, never of this one (a parent waits in `accepting` for every child, and the split part usually builds on this pull request's unmerged code): `dispatch issue --project <project> --parent <parent> --title '<outcome>' --blocked-by <this issue> --spec-file -`, released with `release_children` once this issue closes. On the root, narrow it to what settles here and open a `dispatch ask` for the rest (`skill://legion-worker/references/findings.md`): a person decides whether that becomes an issue. Tell the implementer and the reviewer that this pull request no longer carries it. **Accept the rounds**: each round found less than the one before it, and the last found no defect in the change itself (a stale line, a comment that states an old default); let the loop run and say why in the comment. A later notice is a new decision: accept again only when its round found less than the round you last accepted, and otherwise narrow or split. A finding that changes what the spec accepts is a decision block in the root spec ("After approval" in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`), and `dispatch ask` is only for a decision that needs a human's authority; neither replaces the choice. Judge a worker's backward move by the reason that worker gives. A move you ordered for a conflict found before `awaiting_merge` ("A conflicting head" in `skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`) is a conflict return. The merger's move back to `reviewing` for a READY refusal (`READY_UNREVIEWED_CHANGE`, or `READY_NOT_APPROVED` when its task names no approved head), and the reviewer's no-fix request for changes that follows it so the tester runs the head, are not the work going in circles either. A move that sends back code found wrong counts with the tester fails. |
| `pr-merged` | The pull request merged and the issue did not close; its `reason` says why. Merged before the issue reached `awaiting_merge`: the workflow runs on, and the merge closes the issue the moment it gets there, your acceptance and retro first. Merged while the rest of the tree still runs: the reason names the pull request and every issue the close waits for, and the root waits in `awaiting_merge`, slot and workers kept, until the last of them is done or parked ("The close" in `skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`). Either way you start nothing and no further notice follows. |
| `pr-closed-unmerged` | Decide from current scope whether the work is reopened, started over (`park_child` then `rerun_child`, for a child), or ended with a reason. Delegate the repository action to the responsible phase worker and keep ownership. |
| `merge-refused` | GitHub refused the daemon's submission of READY's merge in a way a retry would meet again, so it submits that merge no further: ask a person with `dispatch ask` to fix the repository setting or App permission and merge at READY's head (`skill://legion-architect/references/merge-notices.md#merge-refused`). |
| `merge-stalled` | GitHub shows the pull request closed, or at another head, while the record still awaits READY's head, and that change's webhook never reached the daemon: read the pull request on GitHub and act on what it shows (`skill://legion-architect/references/merge-notices.md#merge-stalled`). |
| `merge-removed` | A person, or GitHub on its own, took the pull request out of the merge queue or turned its auto-merge off at READY's head after the daemon submitted the merge: read the pull request on GitHub and act on the removal's reason (`skill://legion-architect/references/merge-notices.md#merge-removed`). |
| `merge-waiting` | GitHub has not completed the merge the daemon submitted within an hour of READY's acceptance, and nothing has been withdrawn: read the pull request on GitHub for what GitHub waits on and ask a person for what no worker can do (`skill://legion-architect/references/merge-notices.md#merge-waiting`). |
| A reply on an ask you follow | Interpret the reply in the issue's design context. Answer in its thread (`dispatch comment --reply-to <comment id>`; under an open ask whose next move is yours, such as the approval request you must revise or hand back, `--reply-to-ask <ask id> --turn agent`, since a default-turn reply hands that request back to the human and a corrected `--summary` is then refused), then adjust the plan or relay it via `envoy_publish` to the responsible worker's role token; scope and product decisions remain with you. |
| `worker-died` | Its `role` and `phase`. That role's claim failed: its launches or prompts ran out. For a phase worker the daemon holds the issue (phase `held`) and starts nothing more on it. Reassess the work, then decide with `retry_or_escalate`: `retry` when the failure looks agent-specific or transient, `escalate` to hand the held issue to the controller when it looks environmental. |
| `held` | Its `role` and `phase` (the phase the issue left), or `phase` with `reason: "escalated"`. Without `reason`, that phase's worker ran out of launches or prompts: the issue is held (phase `held`), the `worker-died` that comes with it is yours to answer with `retry_or_escalate` as that row says, and the controller hears of the hold too. With `reason: "escalated"`, it records your own `escalate`: the controller has the issue now, and you start nothing for it. |
| `ready-refused` | Its `version` and `reason`. The merger's READY was refused, and its packet is kept with the completion so no second READY is ever needed. With `version` greater than 0, `reason` reads `READY refused: approve design version <N> before requesting READY.`: the root's spec is already registered at that version, so get it approved (`skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`), and the daemon advances the merge and posts the kept packet the moment it is. With `version` 0, `reason` reads `READY refused: no design version is approved; register and approve the tree's spec before requesting READY.`: the root has no spec registered yet, so register it and get it approved (`skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`) exactly as above; the daemon releases the kept packet the same way — at registration itself when that opens the gate (`gates.design: off`, or Dispatch already shows the version approved), otherwise the moment a human approves the version you registered. Either way, nothing else is needed once the gate opens. On a root whose catch-up said `carried`, the gate is a copy on the old root's spec document: run nothing of `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`; tell the controller the READY waits on a human's approval of that document, and `carried-gate-opened` follows the approval. A `reason` starting `READY_PACKET_MISSING` names a READY refused before the daemon kept packets: that READY is void and the issue stays in merging, so start it over (`park_child` then `rerun_child`, for a child) or end it. |
