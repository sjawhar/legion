## Wake routing

Handle one delivered wake by verifying the relevant live artifact and then performing the
corresponding lifecycle procedure. Architect-addressed wakes always reach the architect that owns
the payload issue: a claimed child sub-architect, otherwise the nearest claimed ancestor, then the
root. A wake about an architect role's own launch reaches the architect above it. `phase-finished`,
worker lifecycle, child lifecycle, and design-gate wakes are architect-only and never go to an
active phase worker.

A "section N" below is that section of `skill://legion-architect`. The spec and gate steps of
section 1 are in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`; the detail of sections 3, 6
and 7 (a conflict, the hand-over, the close) is in
`skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`.

| Wake | Procedure |
| --- | --- |
| `carried-gate-opened` | Only a root whose catch-up said `carried` gets it. Its `version` and `reason`: a human approved the old root's spec document at `version`, which opened the gate this root carried; the daemon has started any child waiting on it. It is not `design-approved`: do not proceed to section 2 or release a wave because of it, and start nothing. |
| `child-status` | A child of your tree left the workflow or re-entered it, or joined your tree from the admission queue; the reason names the child and its new status, or reads `<KEY> joined the tree of <ROOT> from the admission queue`. `todo` (a human's move, your `release_children`, or your `rerun_child`) means it runs again under your tree from planning, and the daemon starts it; you start nothing. A child held for a blocker starts the same way once nothing holds it, and the reason, naming it and its root, says what cleared the hold: its last blocker's close, naming the issues it waited on, now done, or a person or you taking its blocked-by links off; you start nothing for it either. `backlog`, `icebox` or `triage` (a human's move, or your `park_child`) means the daemon has suspended its workers, and it advances no further until it is set back to `todo`. Joined from the admission queue means a root that waited for a slot and never ran, which a person made a child of your tree or your `release_children` adopted, now runs in your tree with no slot or architect of its own, from planning when it is `todo` and parked otherwise; one that waits on an unfinished blocked-by issue is held for that blocker like any other child, and starts when nothing holds it; you start nothing. What the rest of the tree does is your decision. |
| `child-closed` | Read the child completion and remaining open children. Re-scope open work, park obsolete work for good with `park_child` (first take its link off each sibling that waits on it, or park that sibling too, as section 2 says), and file any new child the result calls for and release it at once with `release_children`, blocked by what it builds on (section 2). When a worker waits on this child for a missing surface (see `phase-finished`), tell it to continue with `envoy_publish` to its role topic. The last child's close is not the end-game (section 3). |
| `child-release-stalled` | Its `reason` names a child your `release_children` released that your tree has not recorded, its parent, and why, one of three: the parent's release has not reached the daemon after three tries; or GitHub did not answer the App installation read of a repository on the child's parent chain, and the wave left the child unrecorded because a parent on that chain is not `todo`, or did not write the child `todo` because its parent is not recorded; or the daemon's recovery of such a repository, once GitHub answered, still left children unrecorded (one notice per tree lists them all, each with its parent). Read the parent's record with the `legion` tool's `read_record` (`issue` the parent); release the parent first when the `reason` says to, then run `release_children` for the child again once the parent is recorded in your tree, and tell the controller if it never is. |
| `child-not-recorded` | Its `reason` names a child your tree did not record and why: its repository resolves to none (neither it nor an ancestor names one, and the project has no default), or to one not mapped to the project, or to one the daemon leaves out for a missing GitHub App. The daemon said why on the child too. Set the child's repository to one of the project's (`dispatch issue-update --repo owner/name`), then release it again with `release_children`; tell the controller when the repository it needs is not served. |
| `design-approved` | Its `version` is the approved one. A human approved the root spec document at its current version; the gate is open. Proceed to section 2 — except on a root whose catch-up said `handed_over`: its workers already run their phases, so start nothing. One whose `version` is the version a human's own answer to a decision block wrote, after an earlier approval (Dispatch carried that approval to it, "After approval" in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`), opens nothing new: carry on, and release no wave because of it. A root that was waiting on a question in `awaiting_person` gets no such notice: the task that resumes it says "Your spec is approved at version N and the design gate is open: this resume is your design-approved wake", and that resume is this wake — proceed to section 2 on it. |
| `design-changes-requested` | Its `version` and `reason`. A human asked for changes to the root spec at `version`, for `reason`. Revise the spec as the reason asks and request approval again as "The armed gate" in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md` says; end your turn; the gate is closed. On a root that was waiting on a question in `awaiting_person`, a review the human gave after the wait began comes in the resume task, not in this notice, whether it answered the wait or came after something else did: the task names the review with its version and reason, unless a later approval superseded it. A `version` below the latest, or a second request for one version, is a request that reached the daemon late: read it as the same section's last paragraph says. Each request arrives once. |
| `phase-finished` | The daemon has already moved the issue to its next phase by its fixed table and started that phase's role; you start nothing. Read the committed handoff for the finishing phase; if it shows unresolved gaps, tell the role now working the issue (`envoy_publish` to its role topic). A `planner` notice that names a departure from the spec's design defers to the full condition in "After approval" of `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`: when the approved Summary, Acceptance, scope and settled decisions still hold, the plan is the record; otherwise change the root spec and request approval again as that section says. A `reviewer` notice whose GitHub review is `CHANGES_REQUESTED` needs nothing from you: the daemon has returned the issue to `implementing` (Dispatch `in_progress`) and started the **implementer**, whose correction goes through the tester and the reviewer again, never straight to retro. A `reviewer` notice with an `APPROVED` review means the issue is in `accepting` and the acceptance is yours (section 6): read the evidence, then `sign_off` or send it back with `request_backward_move`. A `tester` notice with `verdict: "fail"` — its handoff carries `implementerProof.verdict: "rejected"`, or a failure naming the production-like proof — has gone back to the **implementer** by the daemon's table; never supply the proof from another role. A worker that reports no surface reaches the changed path sends that report instead of completing its phase, so the daemon starts nothing more on that issue and the worker stays idle in its session, not suspended: file a child issue in this tree to build the surface (infrastructure, tooling, or a skill), and when that child's `child-closed` arrives, tell the waiting worker to continue with `envoy_publish` to its role topic. That report is never a reason to advance the phase. |
| `pr-blocked` | Its `reason` reads `Issue reached review_round_cap=<N>.`, the implementer's round count: every move back to an earlier phase counts one, and the notice comes again with each round past the cap. It moves nothing, since the round that reached the cap has already started its worker. Tell the rounds apart by what announced each: a tester's fail by its `phase-finished` with `verdict: "fail"`; a request for changes by the reviewer's `phase-finished` over a `CHANGES_REQUESTED` review; the daemon's own move by a `checks-red` notice, whose reason names the conflict it was raised for (`the head conflicts with <base>: GitHub runs no checks on it; merge <base> forward`); a worker's `request_backward_move` by a `phase-finished` from the role that asked, whose reason reaches only the task of the worker the move started, so ask that worker; your own send-back from `accepting` by the reason you gave. Judge the cap by the rounds that are not conflicts. Conflict returns need nothing from you beyond the next round. Tester fails and requests for changes are the rounds the cap is for; a red check moves nothing and is told to nobody as a notice, so it is no round at all. Each `review_round_cap` notice is a decision you make in the turn it wakes, never one left for the next round to make: read what every counted round found, choose one of the three below, act on it in the same turn, and post it as a `dispatch comment` on the issue naming the rounds you read and the choice. **Narrow**: the change itself is sound but each round finds more around it; tell the implementer and the reviewer (`envoy_publish` to each one's role topic) which acceptance lines the next round answers; a correctness finding is still fixed in this pull request, and so is a wording nit or any clear fix in a file it touches, so the `Fast-follow:` line carries only cleanup in files this pull request does not touch. **Split**: part of the work cannot settle in this pull request. On a child, file that part as a child of this issue's parent, never of this one (this issue closes at its own merge, and the split part usually builds on this pull request's unmerged code): `dispatch issue --project <project> --parent <parent> --title '<outcome>' --blocked-by <this issue> --spec-file -`. Release it now with `release_children`: Legion holds it on that `--blocked-by` link until this issue closes, and starts it then. On the root, narrow it to what settles here and open a `dispatch ask` for the rest (`skill://legion-worker/references/findings.md`): a person decides whether that becomes an issue. Tell the implementer and the reviewer that this pull request no longer carries it. **Accept the rounds**: each round found less than the one before it, and the last found no defect in the change itself (a stale line, a comment that states an old default); let the loop run and say why in the comment. A later notice is a new decision: accept again only when its round found less than the round you last accepted, and otherwise narrow or split. A finding that changes what the spec accepts is a decision block in the root spec ("After approval" in `skill://legion-architect/references/design-gate.md`), and `dispatch ask` is only for a decision that needs a human's authority; neither replaces the choice. Judge a worker's backward move by the reason that worker gives. The merger's move back to `retro` for a head that still carries `.legion/<issue>/`, and the retro push that answers it, are not the work going in circles. A move that sends back code found wrong counts with the tester fails. |
| `pr-merged` | The pull request merged and the issue did not close; its `reason` says why. Merged before the issue reached `merging`: the workflow runs on, and the merge closes the issue the moment it gets there, your acceptance and retro first. Merged while the rest of the tree still runs: the reason names the pull request and every issue the close waits for, and the root stays in `merging`, slot and workers kept, until the last of them is done or parked ("The close" in `skill://legion-architect/references/acceptance.md`). Either way you start nothing and no further notice follows. |
| `pr-closed-unmerged` | Decide from current scope whether the work is reopened, started over (`park_child` then `rerun_child`, for a child), or ended with a reason. Delegate the repository action to the responsible phase worker and keep ownership. |
| A reply on an ask you follow | Interpret the reply in the issue's design context. Answer in its thread (`dispatch comment --reply-to <comment id>`; under an open ask whose next move is yours, such as the approval request you must revise or hand back, `--reply-to-ask <ask id> --turn agent`, since a default-turn reply hands that request back to the human and a corrected `--summary` is then refused), then adjust the plan or relay it via `envoy_publish` to the responsible worker's role token; scope and product decisions remain with you. |
| `worker-died` | Its `role` and `phase`. That role's claim failed: its launches or prompts ran out. For a phase worker the daemon holds the issue (phase `held`) and starts nothing more on it. Reassess the work, then decide with `retry_or_escalate`: `retry` when the failure looks agent-specific or transient, `escalate` to hand the held issue to the controller when it looks environmental. |
| `held` | Its `role` and `phase` (the phase the issue left), or `phase` with `reason: "escalated"`. Without `reason`, that phase's worker ran out of launches or prompts: the issue is held (phase `held`), the `worker-died` that comes with it is yours to answer with `retry_or_escalate` as that row says, and the controller hears of the hold too. With `reason: "escalated"`, it records your own `escalate`: the controller has the issue now, and you start nothing for it. |
