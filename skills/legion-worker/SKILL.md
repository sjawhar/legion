# Legion Phase Worker

If this skill disagrees with the code, a command's output or its `--help`, follow those and say which line of this skill is wrong.

You work one phase of one issue in a jj workspace every role of the issue shares; the architect owns the tree. Your role prompt gives the workspace binding, `gh`, the push, the handoff record, `handoff_complete` and how long your session stays; this skill adds the rest.

## Every phase, in this order

1. **Read first.** Read the issue, then each handoff before your phase with `dispatch handoff-read --issue <issue> --phase <word>`, in order: `plan`, `implement`, `test`, `review` (a phase with no handoff answers `HANDOFF_NOT_FOUND`). Read the handoff your task's `Handoff:` line names at its version, with the command that line quotes (`dispatch handoff-read --issue <issue> --phase <word> --version <n>`): that is the record the daemon accepted, and a record written to the phase since does not replace it. Where one disagrees with your memory or a transcript, the record wins: it is the copy that survived. At each later assignment read them again, your own included, since other phases may have recorded since. After a `Your workspace was recreated…` message, anything unpushed is gone, and no file in the workspace says so; the handoffs on the Dispatch issue are kept. A release of an idle pod is the one exception for the workspace: the daemon saves the unpushed tracked work and the next pod restores it, and a message names whatever the restore could not bring back.
2. **Commit only your phase's work**, with `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" split -m "<message>" <paths…>`, each commit holding only your phase's paths, so the next phase sees what you changed.
3. **Put back what you copied in.** Before you write the handoff, run `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" status` and remove or restore every file you copied or fetched in that is not your phase's work, such as another branch's test rig. With jj the working copy is the commit: the next role's first commit builds on what you left, and its push ships it.
4. **Write the handoff** your role prompt names, with the `write` tool to `$TMPDIR/<issue>-<word>.json`, outside the workspace (jj snapshots every file in the workspace into the working-copy commit), then record it with `dispatch handoff-write --issue <issue> --phase <word> --body-file "$TMPDIR/<issue>-<word>.json"`. A handoff is never a commit, and no handoff carries the evidence: what you ran is a result you record against one of the issue's checks with `dispatch result`, and `skill://legion-worker/references/pr-body.md#what-a-result-is` defines a check and a result. To change a handoff already recorded, read it with `dispatch handoff-read`, edit the body, and record it whole again: each `dispatch handoff-write` adds a version, and `handoff_complete` reports the newest.
5. **Push any work, then complete**, as the push section below says. `handoff_complete` is your only way to end a phase: add no label, keep no status of your own and run no loop of your own, since the daemon owns the issue's state and the architect its decomposition and gates.

## Stop rules

- **A refused completion is information, not a retry loop.** Quote the answer verbatim to the architect, so "my work is lost" and "my work belongs to an ended run" read differently. `HANDOFF_STALE_GENERATION` (the issue is on a newer run) or `HANDOFF_NO_RUN` (the daemon can attribute your completion to no run: your claim holds no task of this issue's current run and phase — no task at all, or one of another run, of another phase, or of no phase — and the answer says which of those it holds): push nothing further, end the turn without completing, and tell the architect; if the task then arrives and its work and handoff are already done, call `handoff_complete` again in that task's turn, and never redo the work or write a second handoff. `HANDOFF_STALE_ASSIGNMENT` (the issue left your phase and came back while your turn ran, so your work belongs to the assignment before the one the phase hands you now): end the turn without completing and tell the architect, and report that work and that verdict no further. The phase was entered again, so the task it hands you now is work of its own — a testing assignment after a red is for the head the fix pushed, which your verdict on the head before it does not answer for: do that work at the head the task names, write a handoff of its own for it, and complete in its turn. `HANDOFF_NOT_CURRENT_PHASE`: the issue left your phase; report, never complete again. `HANDOFF_NO_PULL_REQUEST` (the implementer's completion found no open pull request on `legion/<KEY>`, and none recorded merged): open it and complete again at the same commit; when the answer names a closed pull request, the architect decides the phase: tell it, and open or reopen nothing until it answers. `HANDOFF_ALREADY_RECORDED`: the first answer stands; if you did not see it, tell the architect. `HANDOFF_SESSION_MISMATCH` names a record another session wrote: read it, record it again from your session with `dispatch handoff-write`, and complete. A reminder is not a task: after an accepted completion, a second call is refused.
- **A turn that ends with your phase open is reminded**, at the end of every turn for as long as the phase stays open. Answer by completing, or with a WAITING line naming what you wait on, which buys a quiet wait; a turn that ends while background work of your own is still running buys the same quiet with no line at all, since that work wakes you when it finishes. A run of reminders that change nothing tells your architect. Under `runtime: kubernetes` that quiet wait also lets the daemon release your issue's pod, while background work of yours keeps the pod, because your session reports that work to the daemon.
- **A released pod is not a stop.** Under `runtime: kubernetes` the daemon releases your issue's pod once no role of it has a turn in progress or a turn due for ten minutes. Your session, your phase, your place in it and the task you hold are all kept, and your unpushed commits and working-copy change are restored into the next pod, so a release costs a pod start and nothing else. A wait that raises no event of its own wakes you at the backstop alone, 60 minutes after the release, so name the wait in your WAITING line and expect that hour. A release keeps nothing else of the disk — `$TMPDIR`, every ignored file, each role's home, a test database and the CodeGraph index all go with the pod — so put work you must keep on the branch, in a tracked file or in Dispatch. The backstop runs only while your phase is open.
- **The workspace is read-only for you from the moment your phase ends**, whether you ended it by completing or a CI red took it back while your turn ran: no commit, no `jj new`, no edit to a tracked file, in the turn still running when the message reaches you and in every turn after it, until a new task names your phase. Nothing stops that turn for you, so stopping is yours to do: drop the work in hand and leave whatever you have uncommitted. The active phase owns the workspace, and your session is kept, until your issue leaves the workflow, to answer questions and to take your next assignment.
- **An update task comes before your own work.** The daemon sends one when it must replace the pod your issue's roles run in, and your role prompt carries the steps: push every commit you want kept to the issue branch, call the `legion` tool's `ready_for_update` with the round id that task names, and end the turn. The daemon takes your answer for that round alone and refuses one that names another, so read the id from the task you answer. If your `legion` tool has no `ready_for_update` operation, push, then end your last message with a line that starts with WAITING and names the round, then end the turn: the daemon reads that turn's end as your answer. It completes no phase and ends none. After you answer, you wait: start no further work on the issue until the daemon's next task reaches you.
- **Never start another Legion role**: the daemon starts every phase worker.
- **Never end your turn blocked on a decision someone else owns.** Send a product, scope, design, lifecycle or cross-phase decision to the owning architect as your role prompt says, with the issue, phase, verified observation, what you tried and the decision needed; a human-only to-do is a `dispatch ask`. Otherwise proceed: a phase that stops silently holds its issue until someone notices. Three waits stand rather than proceed, each with a WAITING line naming what it waits on: a planner on a decision the plan itself needs, because a plan completed around an open decision carries that gap into every later phase; a reviewer on a half of the round that has no review, which it neither approves nor reads in the agent's place (`skill://legion-worker/references/merge-gate.md`); and any pushing role whose second push still has not carried the pull request onto its commit, which waits rather than hand the next role a commit the pull request does not name (*Every role pushes its own commits*, below).
- **No surface reaches the changed path** is a report to the architect, never a reason to complete (`skill://legion-worker/references/pr-body.md#when-no-surface-reaches-the-changed-path`).
- **Asking another role**: if its topic answers 404, read its handoff with `dispatch handoff-read` instead.

## Every role pushes its own commits

- Before a push, run the identity check (`skill://legion-worker/references/default-branch-check.md`) and then, with the `$default` it set, read your branch's own commit messages: `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" log -r "${default}@origin..@" -T 'description ++ "\n"'` — earlier phases' commits in it are expected; never `ancestors(@, 5)`, which stops at five where one measurement found branches of 12 and 28 commits, and never `description.first_line()`, which shows no trailer. Invent no trailer of your own: the pane's jj appends `Omp-Session:` to every commit the pane's shell makes, in any jj repository, and, for a repository the deployment names, the issue line `Dispatch: <KEY>` beside it, that one scoped to the pane's own workspace. A jj repository you clone or create under that workspace is stamped like the workspace, so a commit you make in one carries this deployment's issue key: clone a repository you will push elsewhere outside `$LEGION_WORKSPACE`, and push no commit made under it to a repository that takes no issue key. Three commits carry neither — one `git` made in the colocated workspace, one another process made outside the pane's shell, and, on the Claude harness, one a subagent's bash made, which may not carry the session's environment file (anthropics/claude-code#46696, which `packages/claude-envoy/hooks/session-hook.ts` records and ships a `dispatch` fallback for, though one measurement on the pinned release found those exports present there). A commit of yours that shows a line short is cured by describing it again, since the trailer is appended when a description is set. A re-describe appends the issue line only where the shell it runs in has the pane's jj config overlay, so it adds nothing in a shell that carries none — a subagent's bash, or a pane whose overlay the writer could not make. Where it adds nothing, write the line yourself: put `Dispatch: $LEGION_ISSUE_TRAILER` into the description as a trailer line of its own, and only when `LEGION_ISSUE_TRAILER` is set, since that variable is the deployment's own name for the key this repository takes, and an unset one means the repository takes no key and the line belongs in no commit of it. Look because a base branch can require the issue line in the commit that lands: a squash builds that message from the branch's commit messages, and it leaves a merge commit's message out, so the line belongs in an ordinary commit.
- Only work is pushed: code, red tests, a forward merge, a rewrite, and the empty commit that carries the pull request onto the commit you pushed (the bullet below). No commit message carries a line or bracket keyword that stops GitHub's workflows (`[skip ci]` and its kin), since every head needs its own checks and a base that requires a check will not merge a head whose checks never ran. Retro pushes nothing: it records what the work taught with `dispatch comment` on the issue.
- **After a push, read the pull request's head and check that it is your commit.** A branch and a pull request are two objects on GitHub: the push moves the branch, and GitHub then moves the pull request onto the new commit, which is what starts its checks, and a push GitHub does not follow leaves the newer commit with no checks while Legion reads and tests the older one. The step applies once the branch has an open pull request, whose number your task's `Pull request:` line names (`gh pr list --repo <the issue's repository> --head legion/<KEY> --state open --json number --jq '.[0].number'` when it names none): run `gh pr view <n> --repo <the issue's repository> --json headRefOid --jq .headRefOid` about ten seconds after your push, again about ten seconds later, and a last time about thirty seconds after that, with a read that gives the pushed commit ending the step there and only the last read deciding a mismatch, and compare each answer with the commit you pushed, which `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" log -r legion/<KEY>@origin --no-graph -T commit_id` prints in the 40 characters `headRefOid` answers in. This read is of the pull request's own head and of no check, so the wait it makes is not a wait on CI (`skill://legion-worker/references/pr-body.md#never-waiting-on-ci`). When the last read still is not your commit, carry the pull request with a commit of its own, an empty one when your work is complete and an empty one just as much when `@` holds work of yours, since a push of a commit the branch already holds moves no ref; the push section of your role prompt gives the one `jj` recipe, which leaves `@` and everything in it where it is. A new head makes every result recorded at the old head older, so record each `before_merge` check again at the new head. When that second push does not carry the pull request either, tell your architect the commit you pushed, the head each read gave and the time of each, and end the turn with a WAITING line naming that answer rather than completing: a phase completed here hands the next role a commit the pull request does not name. The push you make to answer an update task is the exception: that task has you answer the daemon and end the turn at once, so make this read in your next turn, before your phase completes. The merger pushes nothing, so this step never falls to it; it reports a mismatch it sees to the architect.
- A planner whose issue moved back from implementing starts on the remote tip, `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" new legion/<KEY>@origin`, so a commit it makes never sits on the implementer's unpushed commits.

## PR body, review, and the merge gate

- The implementer opens the pull request with the line `Dispatch: <KEY>` in its body, the daemon's link to the issue when the branch name is ambiguous. Later phases push to that branch and edit that pull request, never a new one.
- **No deferrals.** A finding that changes behaviour, hides an error, or breaks a gate is fixed in this pull request, and so is a wording nit or any clear fix in a file it touches: a later round costs a CI pass and a re-read of the whole change. The body's one `Fast-follow:` line reads "none", or names cleanup in files this pull request does not touch.
- **The reviewer fixes a prose-only finding itself** (wording, a count, a tense, a citation) in its own commit, pushed before its review. The exception is the round after a READY refusal. Code or behaviour goes back (`skill://legion-worker/references/merge-gate.md`).
- **The merge gate**, in order: the tester's evidence green → the reviewer's approval of the head → the architect's acceptance → retro → the merger's READY → the merge, which closes the issue → the production check after the close, which the project's `production_check` setting gives to the tester unless it is `off`, and whose every look but the last names its own next look with `wake_at`. When the daemon accepts READY, it submits the merge of READY's head, unless the answer's note begins `MANUAL_MERGE_REQUIRED:`, where a person merges it; no role runs `gh pr merge`.
- Append `<!-- legion: {"session":"<session-id>","phase":"<phase>"} -->` to every pull-request comment and review you post: the daemon finds the session behind a review by it.
- Never write a GitHub issue, by `gh issue` or a non-GET `gh api` on `/issues` (post or edit a PR conversation comment with `gh pr comment`): Legion's issues live on Dispatch. Never run `gh config set` or `gh alias set`, and leave `GH_TOKEN`, `GITHUB_TOKEN` and `GH_HOST` empty: the daemon rewrites your gh directory in place, and a set variable outranks it.
- Before you report a resource unreachable, run the call through the secrets broker's client (`skill://agent-secrets`, which holds the form, the exit statuses and where a name comes from), and check `AWS_CONFIG_FILE` (read its profiles): a deployment can give your pod either. Your `task` subagents and background jobs inherit both, and your `gh` and `git`. Request a secret only to use it for your issue's work.

## Deployment instructions

Deployment instructions in your prompt override this skill where they conflict, except four rules: no deferrals; bringing the base into the branch only on a real conflict, a need only the base meets, a retarget, or a parent's last round (`skill://legion-worker/references/conflicts-and-rewrites.md#reintegrating-the-base`); the implementer's own result for every `before_merge` check, on a production-like surface at the head that merges, an applied simplify head included (`skill://legion-worker/references/pr-body.md#what-a-result-is`, `skill://legion-worker/references/pr-body.md#the-rules-every-phases-evidence-follows`); and the production check after the close, the tester's wherever the project's `production_check` does not say `off` (`skill://legion-worker/references/merge-gate.md#the-production-check-after-the-close`).
