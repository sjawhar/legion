# Plan: long and deeply nested lists, lazy quotes and bracket runs no longer freeze a reader's tab (INFRA-625)

## What a reader loses today, and what changes

Today one comment, message or ask whose body holds a long list, a deeply nested list, a quote followed by lines without `>`, or a long run of `[` and `]` freezes the browser tab of everyone who opens it. Measured at main (f45c6716) in Chromium on the issue pod, for bodies at the 32,000-character body cap: a quote with lazy lines holds the tab for about 22 s, a list item with lazy lines 19 s, a bracket run 10 s, 100 nested list items followed by blank lines 3.7 s, a plain list of 8,000 items about 2 s (1.45 s to parse, 0.5 s to draw). A chain of 1,000 `- ` on one line (2,000 characters) breaks the page's drawing with a stack overflow.

After this change: the five superlinear costs are gone, so every one of those shapes costs time in proportion to its length; a list or quote opened inside 100 blocks reads as the text it is, as Dispatch's document service already refuses it (so no chain overflows or grows quadratic); and a body longer than 4,000 characters is parsed off the page's thread, in a Web Worker, so no body under the cap holds the tab for a second. The reader sees a long body's text at once and its formatting when the worker answers (under about 1.5 s for the heaviest shape on this pod). A document write through the API stays under a second, and Go's reading of every body is unchanged.

## Measurements and causes

All tables, raw logs, patches and harnesses are committed on this branch under `.legion/INFRA-625/spikes/` (`micromark/`, `nesting/`, `go/`, `worker/`, `gen.mjs`, `layers.ts`). Chromium is Playwright's 1.63 Chromium on this 3-core pod; numbers move up to 2× with load, so each before/after pair was taken back to back.

Go (`pmdoc`, CPU time, normal build, best of 21): every shape is already under 0.5 s: `ParseForWrite` of the plain list 400 ms, of 49 nested items with blank lines 340 ms, of the bracket run 480 ms; `:::callout[` + brackets, `- `×16000 and 100 nested items are refused in milliseconds. Under `-race` (CI's `envoy-go-race` job) the same calls take 2-8 s. The plain list's hotspot is `pmdoc/block_ids.go` `mintBlockID` → `uuid.NewString`, one `getrandom` per block (54 % of `Parse`); a tab-indentation check runs on every open block per line (`pmdoc/lines.go:235-240`, 12 %).

No change to the parse makes the plain 8,000-item list parse under a second on the page's thread: after the fixes it is linear and still about 1.4 s in Chromium here, and its 16,001 blocks take another 0.4-0.7 s to serialize and lay out. That is why this plan parses long bodies in a worker (decision 2 below).

## Decisions this plan makes, and the departures from the spec

1. **How A1's "draws on the Conversation tab in under 1 s" is proven.** As INFRA-581 proved its identical wording (`packages/dispatch/e2e/nested-directive.e2e.ts`, accepted at head fc1e2366 of sjawhar/legion#2403): a main-thread heartbeat reset at the first comments request, asserting the longest gap is under 1,000 ms until the card has drawn its formatted body. The new rows also record, as an annotation, the time from that request until the formatted body is in the page. The card shows the body's text at once; the formatted list arrives when the worker answers. *Departure:* the spec's Change 2 asks that each list body "parse in well under a second in all three" engines. The plain 8,000-item list and 100 nested items with blank lines cannot (≈1.4 s and ≈1.5 s after every fix, linear); the plan meets the freeze bound for them by moving their parse off the page's thread, and meets "well under a second" for the lazy-line and bracket shapes in every engine. The architect decides whether the spec's words change.
2. **A third mechanism: a parse worker for long bodies.** The spec offers a parse fix or a bound. This plan does both, and adds a module Web Worker that runs the same read-only engine for bodies over 4,000 characters, on every surface that draws a body (comments, messages, asks, hover cards, margin cards, the Inbox, the live session view), because no parse fix brings the plain list under a second. *Departure, scope added:* the off-main-thread contract below (no flicker on a streaming turn, the reader's place kept across a late paint, no portal into an interim page, a pending marker distinct from the failure marker).
3. **The document editor keeps a linear cost.** The dependency patches and the nesting bound apply to every engine in the page, the default mode and the live editor included (they share `micromark` and its constructs). The worker serves the read-only engine only. A version view (`setMarkdown`) or a paste of an 8,000-item list in the editor still parses on the page's thread in about 1.4 s. *Departure:* "all three" holds for the superlinear shapes and the nesting bound, not for the plain list in the editor. The editor's document is loaded from its stored tree, not by parsing markdown, so opening a document is unaffected.
4. **A comment nested past 100 blocks reads as text past that point.** The browser bound counts exactly what Go's `maxNesting` counts (`pmdoc/nesting.go:16-19`: a quote, a list, a list item, a typed block, a footnote definition each count one), so every document Go accepts reads the same, and a list or quote opener Go would refuse reads as its text in a comment, where today it overflows the stack from about 650 `- `. This mirrors the architect's INFRA-581 decision for `:::` ("past the bound only the excess lines read as text in the browser, and Go refuses the write", dispatch://INFRA-581/comment/8c701d3e). A bound tighter than Go's (about 24 blocks would bring the nested-with-blank-lines shape to 0.5 s on the page's thread) was rejected: it changes how documents Go accepts read.
5. **The Go goldmark fork is dropped.** Normal-build Go already meets the 1 s bound on every shape; the two goldmark fixes measured in the spike (blank-line stats, `containsLink`) would need a fork repository, which the deployment instructions list as an approval-first action ("creating … a repo … on an outside platform"). The pmdoc-only fixes stay.
6. **One HTML character-reference decoder everywhere.** The worker cannot load `decode-named-character-reference`'s browser build (`index.dom.js` calls `document.createElement` at module scope). The page and the worker both resolve it to the package's `index.js` (the table Bun, Node and the pmdoc fixture generator already use), so a body reads the same above and below the threshold and the same as the generator that pins Go's fixtures. Cost: +11.6 KB gzipped on the page bundle.
7. **The threshold is 4,000 characters.** After the fixes the worst cost per character at the cap is about 46 µs in Chromium here (plain list 1.4 s, nested with blank lines 1.47 s, per 32,000 characters), so a 4,000-character body costs at most about 0.2 s on the page's thread: 5× under the bound, 2.5× on a loaded pod. A row at 3,999 characters of the worst shapes proves it.
8. **No body is posted to production.** The production check reads the running build and the page only, as INFRA-581's did; a reader whose tab still runs an old build would freeze on these bodies.
9. **`resolveContentExits` splits into five rule functions and `placeExits`.** One function per bullet of its doc comment (`directive-container.ts:264-297`): a container inside a directive container ended by a line; an unclosed directive container; a closed directive container; the directive container's own exit; a line that opens a directive container first; plus `placeExits`, which moves the exits the rules chose. `resolveContentExits` stays as the driver. Naming and structure only.

## The design

### D1. Four dependency patches (linear time, same output)

Root `package.json` `patchedDependencies`, files under `patches/`, as the four existing patches are (`@milkdown/preset-gfm`, `@milkdown/plugin-clipboard`, `prosemirror-tables`, `nats`). Start from the spike's patches in `.legion/INFRA-625/spikes/micromark/`; each patches both `lib/` and `dev/lib/`, because Vite resolves the `development` export condition. `micromark` stays at 4.0.2 (4.0.3 is quadratic on quotes and lists, micromark/micromark#246).

- `micromark@4.0.2`: `writeToChild`'s lazy-line test scans forward from a watermark of events already known to end before the line; the boolean it answers is unchanged (a skipped event's end is at or before `lineStartOffset`, which only grows for one child flow). The watermark is dropped when the event below it changed (resolvers rewrite events), for a new child flow, and at the end of input.
- `micromark-core-commonmark@2.0.3`: `label-end`'s opening scan resumes where the previous `]` stopped and skips below the label start that `]` balanced; the definition lookup is skipped when no definition exists, and otherwise bails, without slicing, once the label holds more normalised characters than the longest defined identifier. (A 999-character cap is wrong: micromark 4.0.2 matches `[a` + 1,000 spaces + `b]` to `[a b]`.)
- `micromark-extension-gfm-footnote@2.1.0`: the call scan is skipped when no footnote is defined (definitions are flow and all known before text runs) and otherwise resumes as above.
- `mdast-util-from-markdown@2.0.3`: `prepareList` records its list-item enters and exits; `compile` inserts them all in one stable rebuild.

Run `bun install` and commit the lockfile change these entries cause, and only that: a fresh `bun install` in this workspace also rewrote unrelated lines of `bun.lock` during planning.

### D2. A nesting bound for lists and quotes, equal to Go's (`packages/proof-editor/src/block-nesting.ts`, new, legion-owned)

Start from `.legion/INFRA-625/spikes/nesting/block-nesting.patch`. `maxBlockNesting = 100`; its comment names `pmdoc/nesting.go` `maxNesting`, and that constant's comment names it back. A fresh list opener opens two levels (the list and its first item), a later item of an open list opens none new, a quote opens one, and a `:::` container counts one (`directive-container.ts` records its depth into the same count). An opener that would stand past the bound returns `nok` and its line reads as text, the way `directive-container.ts` treats a `:::` past 32. The depth around a line is read from the continuations that succeeded on it plus the line's own events back to the previous `exit chunkFlow`, because micromark runs the first start of a line twice (once inside a `check` it discards). The same module installs a blank-line fast path in list-item continuation (a line of only spaces or tabs takes the continuation without the generic `check(blankLine)`), which cut the nested-with-blank-lines shape by about 35 % with 0 differences.

micromark's `disable` cannot express this (a disabled construct name is refused for every `attempt`, including `list`'s attempts on itself), so the module wraps the `list` and `blockQuote` objects exported by `micromark-core-commonmark` in place, once, idempotently, when `lib-remark-directive-plugin.ts` is imported. Every parser in the page bundle, in the worker bundle and in the pmdoc fixture generator (`pmdoc/gen` imports `@legion/proof-editor/headless`) then counts the same way. A second copy of `micromark-core-commonmark` in the tree would escape the wrap: a proof-editor test asserts the bound holds through `remark-parse` (the path milkdown and the headless engine use), so a duplicate copy fails the build.

Footnote definitions are not counted in the browser (Go counts them). That only lets the browser draw deeper than Go would accept, never shallower, so parity holds; say so in the module's header.

### D3. A parse worker for bodies over 4,000 characters (`packages/dispatch/web/src/features/refs/`)

- `markdown-parse.worker.ts` (new): loads the block schema it is given, builds `createHeadlessProof({ blockSchema, readOnly: true })` (no `renderBlock`: it is read only by `toDOM`, `block-schema.ts:197`), and answers each request `{ id, markdown, softBreaks }` with `doc.toJSON()` posted as a plain object (structured clone measured no slower than a JSON string), or a refusal when the parser throws. When the body holds a ```` ```mermaid ```` fence, the same answer carries the open-fence probe that `mermaid-diagrams.ts:146-170` computes today with a second parse, so a streaming turn costs one round trip per frame, not two. It posts `{ ready: true }` once its imports load.
- `markdown-engine.ts`: `renderWithEngine` becomes a call that takes the source and soft-break policy and calls back with the engine and the parsed document. A source of at most 4,000 characters is parsed synchronously, exactly as today. A longer one is sent to the worker (created on first use, not by `warmMarkdownRenderer`, so a page without a long body downloads nothing more), and the answer is turned into a node with `schema.nodeFromJSON`. Answers are cached by schema version, policy and source (a few entries, newest kept), so a change of `Card`, `paint` or `decorate` repaints without another round trip. The deadline starts when the worker can start on the job, as `mermaid-diagrams.ts` does: 10 s, after which the worker is terminated and replaced and the body stays as its literal text with `data-markdown-fallback`, the existing terminal state. The worker factory is a module-level seam that tests replace.
- `useRenderedMarkdown.tsx`: while a long body is pending, a first paint writes its literal text and sets `data-markdown-pending="true"` (new; `data-markdown-fallback` keeps meaning a final failure, as nine tests assert); an update of an element already painted keeps its previous paint and marks it pending, so a streaming turn never flips to source text. Each request carries a sequence number and only the newest answer paints. Every paint, pending or final, recomputes the ask hosts, reference anchors and picture anchors in the same callback, so no portal points into a replaced page. `onRendered` fires on the final paint. `useMarkdownHeadline` takes the same path (it is already state-based).
- **The reader's place across a late paint.** `ViewportAnchor` measures once per commit (`shell/ViewportAnchor.tsx:186-217`) and `use-follow-latest.ts:53-66` places once, so a body that formats after its commit must not move the reader: when a final paint replaces a pending one, the hook measures the element's top and height before and after; if the element lies wholly above the scroller's visible top, it scrolls by the height change, unless the browser's own scroll anchoring already moved the page (compare the anchor row's position, so the shift is never applied twice); and when Jump to latest placed the reader and its newest turn is the one that just formatted, it places again once. Proven by rows in Chromium and WebKit (below).
- **Layout of a long list.** A body painted from the worker path gets the class `dispatch-markdown-long`; `styles.css` gives its `li` `content-visibility: auto; contain-intrinsic-size: auto 1.5em`. On a bare page this cut the layout of 8,000 items from 222-387 ms to 82-88 ms. The implementer measures it in the app: if the plain-list row's main-thread gap at the head, on an idle pod in Chromium, is above 600 ms with it, the painter appends a long body's blocks (top-level blocks, and a list's items) in slices of at most 50 ms of work per task instead, and drops the CSS. The rows assert counts of `li`, not their visibility, since a skipped subtree has no box.
- **The worker bundle.** Vite bundles the worker with the client conditions, so `decode-named-character-reference` resolves to `index.dom.js` and the worker dies at load. `web/vite.config.ts` aliases the package to its `index.js` for the page and the worker (decision 6). A throwaway differential decodes every name in `character-entities` plus malformed references (no `;`, unknown names, numeric edge cases) with both builds in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox; its result goes in the pull request body.

### D4. Go (`packages/envoy/internal/dispatch/pmdoc`)

From `.legion/INFRA-625/spikes/go/pmdoc.diff`: `block_ids.go` mints version-4 ids from a 4 KiB `crypto/rand` buffer (256 ids per read, same format, a mutex around the refill) instead of one `uuid.NewString` read per block; `lines.go` skips `expandTabIndentation` in `tabIndented.Open`/`Continue` when the source holds no tab. Trees stay byte-identical (258 fixture, corpus and edge documents hashed identically in the spike). No goldmark change (decision 5).

### D5. `resolveContentExits` (`packages/proof-editor/src/directive-container.ts`)

Decision 9. Each rule function carries its bullet as its doc comment; the driver's comment keeps the linear-time argument.

## What this plan does not change, and why

- **The editor's plain-list cost** (decision 3).
- **Bodies over 32,000 characters.** Documents up to 1 MiB and ask answers get the linear-time fixes and the bound, and read-only surfaces parse them in the worker; their absolute cost is not bounded here. An 8,000-item list weighs about 56,000 of a document's 65,536 elements, so a longer list document is refused by `MaxDocumentElements`, not slowed.
- **goldmark's own per-line costs under `-race`** (decision 5): 2-4 s CPU for the nested shapes in the race build only, where the Go test asserts linear growth instead of a time.
