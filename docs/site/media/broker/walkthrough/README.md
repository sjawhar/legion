# The broker walkthrough's sources

Everything `docs/site/public/media/broker/walkthrough.mp4` and its captions (`walkthrough.vtt`) and
poster frame (`walkthrough.jpg`) are built from, and how to build them again. These sources sit
beside the scripts that record them rather than under `public/`, which the site publishes as it is.

| Path | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `raw/` | The footage, one file per section: four asciinema casts of the agent machine's terminal and three Playwright recordings of the browser (Dispatch, and the broker's own approval page in the middle one), with `sections.json`: each section's wall-clock length, its marks (named on-screen events, in the file's own seconds), and how closely each browser section's last frames match its result. |
| `edl.py` | The cut: which window of which file plays, in what order, and the narration parts laid over each clip, each placed at a mark plus an offset. |
| `narration.json` | Each narration part's text, in speaking order: `narrate.ts` speaks it, and `edl.py` loads it for the captions `build.py` writes. |
| `narration/` | One MP3 per narration part, generated by `narrate.ts`. |
| `narrate.ts` | Generates `narration/` through ElevenLabs, in the voice every docs video uses: `NARRATION_VOICE` and `narrate` from `docs/site/media/narration.ts`. |
| `build.py` | Checks the footage and the cut, renders each clip, concatenates them into `build/walkthrough.mp4` with the narration normalized to -16 LUFS, checks that, writes its captions and poster beside it, and only then copies all three to the published `walkthrough.mp4`, `walkthrough.vtt` and `walkthrough.jpg`. |

## Rebuild the video

Needs `agg` (`cargo install --git https://github.com/asciinema/agg`), `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe`.

```bash
cd docs/site/media/broker/walkthrough
python3 build.py --check   # the footage's capture rates, every mark and window inside its file, every narration part and its caption inside its clip
python3 build.py           # builds build/walkthrough.mp4 and checks it, writes its captions and poster, then copies all three to docs/site/public/media/broker/
```

`build.py` refuses footage whose length is off its section's wall clock (a browser recording
shorter than its actions or longer than its page's life, by more than 5%; a cast more than 3 s
shorter than its section, which ends at the detach that ends the cast, or longer), a clip or part
naming a mark its section did not record, a
window outside its file, a cast's clip that ends within 0.2 s of the cast's last event, a narration
part that starts before its clip, overlaps the next or runs past its clip's end, and a part whose
text in `narration.json` is missing or blank. A cast's last event is the detached client's
farewell, which clears the screen to `[detached (from session agent)]`, and agg's frames can show
it on a clip's last frame or two, so `build.py` places a cast's `end` mark 0.2 s before it
(`FAREWELL_GUARD`): a clip that plays to the end of a cast ends at `At("end")`.
Nothing is stretched or held: a clip is its window of the file, at its own speed, and the audio is
padded with silence, never the video with frames. It prints the cut it resolved (each clip's
window in its file and where each part falls in the video). It writes the video into `build/`,
with the video copied from the clips untouched and the narration normalized to every docs video's
loudness, as `docs/site/media/walkthrough.ts` sets it (`loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5`): -16 LUFS
integrated, with a true peak of at most -1.5 dBTP.
loudnorm does that in two passes over the joined clips, the first measuring and the second
normalizing from that measurement; it aims the peak at -2 dBTP, since encoding the AAC raises it
again. Where the gain would push the peak over its aim, as in this cut, loudnorm normalizes
dynamically rather than by one gain. The build fails the video on loudness more than 0.5 LU off
-16 LUFS or peaking over -1.5 dBTP, and on dead air: 2 s or more of silence over a frozen frame
(ffmpeg's `silencedetect` and `freezedetect`, with silences a blip under 0.3 s apart counted as
one).

A video that passes gets its captions and poster, as the site's other videos have them.
`walkthrough.vtt` holds one WebVTT cue per narration part: the part's text in `narration.json`,
from where the part starts in the video until the next part in its clip starts, or the clip ends.
Each clip starts where the clips before it end, at their rendered lengths, which a frame or an
audio packet can take a few hundredths of a second past their cut lengths, and the whole video
starts 23 ms late, the first clip's AAC priming, which `build.py` reads off the first frame's
start. `walkthrough.jpg` is the video's first frame: the opening payoff, the command's line once
the key reached it. Only then are the three copied to `docs/site/public/media/broker/`; a failed
build leaves the published ones as they were.

## Change the narration

Edit the part's text in `narration.json`, regenerate that part (or several, or all of them
when no part is named), and rebuild. `narrate.ts` reads the ElevenLabs key from
`ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` in its environment, however it got there; `secrets` is one way to supply it:

```bash
ELEVENLABS_API_KEY=<key> bun narrate.ts grants-revoke
secrets ELEVENLABS_API_KEY -- bun narrate.ts grants-revoke
python3 build.py
```

Write a part to the clip as cut: say only what the clip shows, and keep it inside the clip's length
(`build.py --check` names a part that overruns). A part's place is its `At` in `CLIPS`: a mark in
the clip's source and an offset from it.

## Record the footage again

`bash docs/site/media/broker/walkthrough.sh` records every section into `raw/` against the demo
rig (`docs/site/media/broker/README.md`), with the rig's `DATABASE_URL`. A take records all of
them, in order: each section depends on the one before it (the browser types the code the
terminal printed, the session needs the login the browser approved, the browser approves the
request the terminal made, and the terminal records through that approval).

As it records, each section marks its on-screen events by name (`mark("code")` in
`walkthrough.record.ts`, once the login code is on screen), and `sections.json` records each mark
in its file's own seconds: a terminal mark by how long before the cast's last event (the detached
client's farewell) it was set, a browser mark by how long after the page was created, which is
where Playwright's recording starts. Since `edl.py` places everything at marks, a new take re-times
the cut itself: run `python3 build.py --check`, then `python3 build.py`, then watch the result and
adjust an offset only where the new take's pacing calls for it. A cut that should land on a moment
no mark names gets a new `mark(...)` in the recorder and a new take.
