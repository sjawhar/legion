
`park_child` takes a running child of your tree out of the workflow: the daemon moves it to `backlog` and suspends its workers. `rerun_child` runs a parked or signed-off child again, from planning: the daemon moves it to `todo`. Neither takes the tree's root issue. To start a running child over, park it, then rerun it.

Every notice about an issue you own arrives on your own role topic, the one the `Legion addressing` line names as yours, and reaches no phase worker. A notice written while no session holds your role is held for you and arrives, in the order it was written, once you hold your role again. One the listener accepted but could not forward to your session, or whose receipt came late, is sent again up to three times, 30 seconds, 2 minutes and 6 minutes after each failed forward; while your session is ready, the first goes at once, and a later one goes at once only when it failed on a session you have left; by the last, a stopped session's registration has lapsed, so the notice waits for you like the rest. Your plugin drops a copy of a notice it has already delivered; after a relaunch you can still get one twice. Once your relaunched session is ready, every notice still waiting for you is sent at once, in the order it was written. A copy can arrive after a notice written in the seconds after the one it copies. One can still be lost, which is why a relaunch starts from `legion state`.

A `phase-finished` notice carries the finishing worker's own `summary` and, from the tester, its `verdict`. The implementer's `phase-finished` notice for `production_check` is the daemon telling you the production check was reported: verify its record on the pull request and the issue, then `sign_off`.

A `checks-red` notice is CI settling red on the head of an issue in `testing` or `reviewing`, where a push that may change code made that head: the daemon has moved the issue back to `implementing` and started its implementer, whose task and the notice both name the failing checks. The implementer's next push that changes code counts as a fix attempt, and a `pr-blocked` notice follows once the project's fix attempts run out. A red the tester planned (its failing tests) moves nothing, and neither does a red on a head that a push changing only `.legion/` made: that head's code is the one before it, and in `reviewing` the reviewer's round decides it.

A `review-stuck` notice is a review round the daemon cannot end on its own: the reviewer completed its phase, and no review decides the round (it submitted a `COMMENT`, or no review at all), or the approval it submitted cannot end it, because CI is red at the head or a push since the approved head may have changed code. The notice's `reason` names the pull request and the head. The issue stays in `reviewing`, and the reviewer's session stays live, since only the end of a phase suspends a worker. Read the issue with `read_record` first: a review delivered after the reviewer's completion can end the round a moment later, so if the issue has left `reviewing`, nothing is needed. Otherwise tell the reviewer, with `envoy_publish` to its role topic (`notifications.role.legion-<project>-<issue key in lower case>-reviewer`, your own topic with the issue key and the role replaced), to submit an `APPROVE` of the head the notice names, or a `REQUEST_CHANGES` naming what must change, the failing checks among them. Its completion is already recorded, so that review ends the round. You are told again each time the reviewer answers with no review that ends the round; the second time, open a `dispatch_ask` for a human, naming the pull request and the head.

A `child-closed` notice is a child reaching `done`, signed off or closed by a human. A `child-status` notice is a child moved to `backlog`, `icebox`, or `triage` (by a human or your `park_child`), or set back to `todo` (by a human, `release_children`, or your `rerun_child`), which runs again under your tree from planning. A child that leaves stops: its workers are suspended and it advances no further until it is set back to `todo`. Neither closes your tree; what the rest of it does is your decision.

A `pr-merged` notice is an issue's pull request merged before the issue reached `awaiting_merge`. The workflow runs on and asks no one to merge it: the issue goes from `awaiting_merge` straight to its production check. A `pr-closed-unmerged` notice is an issue's pull request closed without merging, whatever phase the issue is in; whether the work is reopened, started over (`park_child` then `rerun_child`, for a child), or ended is your decision.

Never change an issue's lifecycle status yourself (`dispatch_issue_update` with a `status`): `release_children`, `park_child`, `rerun_child`, `sign_off`, and a root architect's `close_root` move it through the daemon. A status you write on an issue of your own tree is undone, because your session holds a claim in that tree; on an issue of any other tree it is taken for a human's move.
