
`park_child` takes a running child of your tree out of the workflow: the daemon moves it to `backlog` and suspends its workers. `rerun_child` runs a parked or done child again, from planning: the daemon moves it to `todo`. Neither takes the tree's root issue. To start a running child over, park it, then rerun it.

Every notice about an issue you own arrives on your own role topic, the one the `Legion addressing` line names as yours, and reaches no phase worker. A notice written while no session holds your role is held for you and arrives, in the order it was written, once you hold your role again. One the listener accepted but could not forward to your session, or whose receipt came late, is sent again up to three times, 30 seconds, 2 minutes and 6 minutes after each failed forward; while your session is ready, the first goes at once, and a later one goes at once only when it failed on a session you have left; by the last, a stopped session's registration has lapsed, so the notice waits for you like the rest. Your plugin drops a copy of a notice it has already delivered; after a relaunch you can still get one twice. Once your relaunched session is ready, every notice still waiting for you is sent at once, in the order it was written. A copy can arrive after a notice written in the seconds after the one it copies. One can still be lost, which is why a relaunch starts from `read_record`.

A `phase-finished` notice carries the finishing worker's own `summary` and, from the tester, its `verdict`. The reviewer's `phase-finished` notice over an `APPROVED` review is the daemon telling you the issue is in `accepting`, where no worker runs and the acceptance is yours: read the evidence (the tester's handoff, the pull request body's `E2E (implementer)` and `E2E (tester)` lines, and the reviewer's approval of the head by SHA), then `sign_off`, which runs only in `accepting` (`SIGNOFF_OUTSIDE_ACCEPTING` elsewhere) and moves the issue to `retro`, or send it back with `request_backward_move` (`issue`, `to` `implementing` or `testing`, `reason`), which starts that phase's role with the reason and counts a review round. Nothing beneath the issue holds its acceptance: you accept the issue's own reviewed head while its children still run, and the daemon keeps the tree's order at the close, where a root whose pull request merged waits for the rest of its tree (the `pr-merged` notice below). A `handoff_complete` from you in `accepting` is refused (`ACCEPTANCE_IS_SIGN_OFF`). The merge closes the issue and the implementer's production check after the close asks nothing of you, so no notice follows the merge for you except the `pr-merged` one below, when the close waits for the rest of your tree.

A `checks-red` notice is CI red on the head of an issue in `testing`, `reviewing`, `accepting`, `retro` or `merging`, by a check or workflow the base branch requires, or on the head of an issue in `awaiting_merge` by that head's own checks and workflow runs, or because its head conflicts with its base - GitHub computes no merge ref for a conflicting head and runs no checks on it at all, so no CI result would ever arrive for it, and the reason then names the base to merge forward rather than a check or workflow - where no round is open to decide it: the daemon has moved the issue back to `implementing` and started its implementer, whose task and the notice both name the red checks or workflows, or the conflicting base. In `testing`, `reviewing` and `merging` the tester, a reviewer that had not completed its round, or the merger did not complete its phase, so the daemon interrupted its turn and started the implementer only once that turn was over; the interrupted role stays live like any finished one. In `accepting` no worker holds the phase, so nothing is interrupted and your acceptance is no longer asked: the round runs again. In `retro` the phase was the implementer's own, so nothing was interrupted: the implementer keeps its turn and is handed the implementing task once it ends, and a retro completion it starts once the issue has moved is refused (the record names `implementing`, whose handoff is the one `handoff_complete` looks for), while one already in flight when the red landed can be taken as the implementing completion, which the tester's own CI read then catches. From `awaiting_merge` the work returns through testing, review and READY, and the READY already posted no longer stands: GitHub will not merge a red or conflicting head, and the daemon has posted the READY's withdrawal on the issue and told the project's merge queue role, when one is set. A merge the merge queue fails comes the same way: once the daemon's submission has put READY's head in the queue, GitHub taking it out because the merge failed (its reason `failed_checks`, or an unexplained reason with a check that failed, timed out, failed to start, needed action or went stale on the commit the queue built for its `merge_group` CI; the runs a person's dequeue cancels are no failure) moves the issue from `awaiting_merge` back to `implementing` rather than queueing the head again, and the reason, which the implementer's task carries too, starts `the merge queue took pull request #<n> out at READY's head` and names each failing check with its run. A red carried to the READY head from the head before it sends nothing back, since that red stood before the READY was accepted and says nothing new about the commit the daemon's submission names, and neither does a red only a required workflow makes at the READY head until the daemon's own read of the runs confirms it. CI is red at a head only when a check or workflow the base branch requires (its rulesets' required status checks and required workflows, and its branch protection's required status checks: the set the daemon's own pass over every open pull request reads, every two minutes, and nothing else does) failed; a check the base branch does not require never makes a head red, a base branch that requires none has no red CI, and until the daemon has read the base branch's required set no verdict stands for the head. A required check that was cancelled, or that the head's checks settled without, leaves no verdict until a later settlement of the head decides it, since a settlement can come before an aggregator job is queued or just after a run is cancelled. A required workflow is judged by its latest run on the head, which the daemon reads every two minutes: a run that failed, was cancelled or timed out is red (one that ended neutral or skipped passes), except a run cancelled on the head before a push that changed only `.legion/`, which that push's own run superseded; one still running leaves no verdict until the next read, and so does a head with no run of it. GitHub starts no workflow run for a head whose commit message carries the `skip-checks` trailer, so a handoff-only push before retro gets no CI of its own, while retro's push, which carries no such line, gets GitHub's workflow runs of its own. The daemon's own carry is keyed to paths, not to that line: it classifies a push as handoff-only when every path it changed starts with `.legion/`, reads no commit message at all, and so carries the code head's verdict to the head any such push made, trailer or not, and a red carried that way is that code's red and sends the work back as the code head's own red would. Retro's deletion-only head therefore both carries the code head's verdict, by its paths, and has check runs of its own, and a red in those settles like any other red. The reviewer's round decides no CI, and its approval of the head ends the round whatever CI says there, since the reviewer reads the code commit's checks on GitHub before it approves (the required set from the base branch's rulesets and branch protection, the head of the branch's newest `pull_request` run from the runs API, that commit's `gh api repos/<owner>/<repo>/commits/<ci head>/check-runs`, and `gh api repos/<owner>/<repo>/compare/<ci head>...<head>` proving only `.legion/` changed since; its role prompt spells the read out). The implementer's next push that changes code counts as a fix attempt, a retro push onto the red that changes a path outside `.legion/` (its `docs/solutions/` commit) among them, and a `pr-blocked` notice follows once the project's fix attempts run out. A red the tester planned (its failing tests) moves nothing before `awaiting_merge`, and neither does a red the fix-attempt cap holds (below). A red that already stands when the reviewer's approval lands is unsent, and the issue is in `accepting`, where the acceptance is yours: read the head's checks on GitHub before you accept, and send a red head back with `request_backward_move`; retro's own push runs CI, a red settlement of which sends the work back under this rule, and GitHub will not merge a head whose required check is red, so the merge waits.

A `pr-blocked` notice, for `max_fix_attempts` or for `review_round_cap`, is handled as the `pr-blocked` row of `skill://legion-architect`'s wake routing says. Once `max_fix_attempts` runs out, CI red within the attempt that ran it out - the head whose push was counted, and every head pushed on top of it until a push lands on a head CI judged green or on a settled head whose base branch requires no check and no workflow - sends nothing back from `testing`, `reviewing`, `retro` or `merging` by itself, so no `checks-red` for CI follows the `pr-blocked` within that attempt: the issue stays in its phase until you move it, or a worker's own decision does. Every push except one onto green, or onto such a settled head, stays in that attempt, the tester's red tests and handoff onto the held red included. A head pushed onto green CI, or onto a settled head whose base branch requires no check and no workflow, leaves the attempt, so its red still comes as a `checks-red`, and so does a red on a `READY` head or a conflict with the base, at any count.

A `review-stuck` notice is a review round the daemon cannot end on its own: the reviewer completed its phase, and no review decides the round (it submitted a `COMMENT`, or no review at all, or an APPROVE or REQUEST_CHANGES the daemon set aside because its Legion footer did not name the session recorded on the reviewer's claim (the daemon logs `workflow: a review decides nothing: the review App submitted it from a session that is not the reviewer's`)), or the approval it submitted is of a head whose code the current head may not carry, because a push since the approved head may have changed code. CI is no cause: the round reads no verdict, and a red at the reviewer's head is the `checks-red` rule above. Its `reason` names the pull request and the head, its `topic` is the reviewer's role topic, and its `summary` names the fact that wrote it: `the reviewer's completion`, `the reviewer's review`, `a push`, or `a review that is not the reviewer's answer`. The issue stays in `reviewing`, and the reviewer's session stays live, as every role's does until its issue closes. Read the issue with `read_record` first: a review delivered after the reviewer's completion can end the round a moment later, so if the issue has left `reviewing`, nothing is needed. Otherwise tell the reviewer, with `envoy_publish` to the notice's `topic`, to submit an `APPROVE` of the head the notice names, or a `REQUEST_CHANGES` naming what must change, a red its own read of the code commit's checks found among them. Its completion is already recorded, so that review ends the round. A notice written by `the reviewer's completion` or `the reviewer's review` after you asked in this same review round is the reviewer answering with no review that ends the round: open a `dispatch ask` for a human, naming the pull request and the head. A request you made in an earlier round, which a request for changes or an approval ended, does not count. A notice written by anything else is news about the round, not an answer: handle it as you did the first.

Every phase worker stays live from its role's first assignment until its issue closes, so a role that finished its phase still answers questions about its work through `envoy_publish` to its role topic, without becoming the active phase. A `worker-died` notice whose `reason` says the role does not work the issue's phase is such a finished role whose relaunches ran out: nothing is held and the phase runs on, so there is nothing to `retry_or_escalate`; read that role's committed handoff instead of asking it, and the next start of the role, for a later phase or a backward move, relaunches its session. A `worker-died` that comes with a `held` notice is the phase's own worker.

A `child-closed` notice is a child reaching `done`: closed by its pull request's merge, or by a human. A `child-status` notice is a child moved to `backlog`, `icebox`, or `triage` (by a human or your `park_child`), or set back to `todo` (by a human, `release_children`, or your `rerun_child`), which runs again under your tree from planning, or a root that waited in the admission queue and never ran joining your tree once it became your child (its reason reads `<KEY> joined the tree of <ROOT> from the admission queue`), which runs in your tree with no slot or architect of its own. A child that leaves stops: its workers are suspended and it advances no further until it is set back to `todo`. Neither closes your tree; what the rest of it does is your decision.

A `pr-merged` notice is an issue's pull request merged where the merge did not close the issue, and its reason says which case it is. **Merged before the issue reached `awaiting_merge`:** the workflow runs on and asks no one to merge it, the merge closes the issue the moment it reaches `awaiting_merge`, your acceptance and retro running first as they would. A merge before retro's removal carries the issue's `.legion/<issue>/` (`<issue>` is the notice's issue) onto the pull request's base, and nothing in the workflow removes it there, while one during `merging` already carried none. Read the pull request (`gh api repos/<owner>/<repo>/pulls/<number> --jq '{base: .base.ref, by: .merged_by.login}'`), then whether its base still holds the directory (`gh api 'repos/<owner>/<repo>/contents/.legion/<issue>?ref=<base>'`): GitHub's 404 means it does not; any other failure leaves it unknown, so read again rather than conclude it is gone. When the base holds it, ask the person who merged (`merged_by`) with `dispatch ask` to merge a pull request that deletes it. **Merged while the rest of the tree still runs:** the reason names the pull request and every issue the close is waiting for, and the root stays in `awaiting_merge` with its admission slot, its architect and its workers; no member is suspended and no linger starts, the children run on to their own merges, and the root closes once the last of them is done or parked. That notice comes once: none follows as those issues leave, and a daemon restart repeats none, so read the pull request on GitHub as you read merge state everywhere else. A `pr-closed-unmerged` notice is an issue's pull request closed without merging, whatever phase the issue is in; whether the work is reopened, started over (`park_child` then `rerun_child`, for a child), or ended is your decision.

A `merge-refused` notice is GitHub refusing the daemon's submission of an accepted READY's merge in a way a retry would meet again (squash merges disabled, a permission the implement App lacks); its reason names the pull request, the READY head, what was asked and GitHub's message. The daemon has stopped submitting that merge and the issue stays in `awaiting_merge`. The cause is a repository setting or an App permission, a person's to change: ask one with `dispatch ask`, naming the refusal and the pull request at the READY head, which that person merges under the repository's rules once it is fixed. Never bypass a rule.

A `merge-stalled` notice is the daemon's submission of an accepted READY's merge ending because GitHub showed the pull request closed, or at another head, while the record awaited READY's head, and GitHub's last change to it was more than 10 minutes old: that change's webhook never reached the daemon. Its reason names the pull request, what GitHub shows and since when, and READY's head. The daemon has stopped submitting that merge and the issue stays in `awaiting_merge`. Read the pull request on GitHub: open at READY's head, a person merges it under the repository's rules; closed, or at another head, ask a person with `dispatch ask`, naming the pull request, what GitHub shows, and READY's head. Never bypass a rule.

A `merge-removed` notice is a person, or GitHub on its own, taking an accepted READY's pull request out of the merge queue, or turning its auto-merge off, at READY's head after the daemon submitted it, for a reason other than a failed merge (a person's dequeue, a person turning auto-merge off, GitHub turning it off itself over a repository setting such as a merge method the repository blocks, the queue finding a conflict); its reason names who removed it, or says GitHub did on its own and names the App that submitted the merge, which GitHub names as the actor of its own disables, then what was removed, GitHub's reason, when, the pull request and READY's head. The daemon submits that merge no further, so the removal stands, and the issue stays in `awaiting_merge`. Read the pull request on GitHub. A `merge_conflict` removal conflicts with the base or with a pull request queued ahead of it: once the conflict is on the base, the daemon's own conflict check sends the issue back to `implementing` by itself. Otherwise ask a person with `dispatch ask` (the one who removed it, when the reason names a person rather than GitHub on its own), naming the pull request at READY's head and the reason; that person merges or queues it again under the repository's rules when it should land, and the merge closes the issue. Never bypass a rule.

A `merge-waiting` notice is an accepted READY's merge the daemon submitted that GitHub has not completed within an hour of the workflow queueing it; its reason names the pull request, READY's head, how long the row has waited and what GitHub last answered. Nothing has been withdrawn: the merge stands, the issue stays in `awaiting_merge`, and the daemon goes on submitting it, so there is nothing to undo and you are told once however much longer it waits. Read the pull request on GitHub and find what GitHub is waiting for: a required check that never ran, a required check that failed, a review only a person can give, or a merge queue that has not landed it. Act on what you find; in `awaiting_merge` no worker holds a phase and a backward move is refused, so what a check, a review or a repository rule needs is a person's: ask one with `dispatch ask`, naming the pull request at READY's head and what GitHub waits on. The merge closes the issue when GitHub completes it, whoever unblocks it. Never bypass a rule.

A `status-reasserted` notice is a session with no claim in your tree writing a lifecycle status on your running root, or on your root while it waits on a person in `awaiting_person` (that notice is held until you resume); its reason names the session, the status it wrote, and the status the daemon set back. The tree runs on and nothing needs undoing. When the write reads as a wish to stop the tree, ask a person with `dispatch ask`: a running tree leaves the workflow only by a person's move in the Dispatch dashboard or by the daemon's own write, the controller's `set_status` or the operator's.

Never change an issue's lifecycle status yourself (`dispatch issue-update` with a `--status`): `release_children`, `park_child`, `rerun_child`, `sign_off`, and a root architect's `close_root` move it through the daemon. A status you write on an issue of your own tree is undone, because your session holds a claim in that tree. On another tree's running root it is undone too, and that root's architect gets a `status-reasserted` notice; on a child of another tree, or a root waiting for the first slot of its generation or lingering, it is taken for a person's move. You write one lifecycle status yourself: the controller's report issue, when no controller answers and that issue is closed or missing. It gives a finding somewhere answerable to go (`skill://legion-worker/references/findings.md`, "When no controller takes it"). The report issue is in no tree and carries no `legion` label, so the daemon re-asserts nothing on it. `sessionStatusWrite` acts only for a session that claims the issue's tree, or for an admitted root.

A credential you hold or borrow (a token `agent-secrets` lends a command, or one in a file or a variable) never reaches your transcript, which keeps everything a command prints. Check its shape by its length and its prefix alone, printing only the number and whether the prefix matches, and compare two by printing only whether they are equal: never print, echo, slice or diff a credential's characters. A secret `agent-secrets` lends exists only in the environment of the command it runs, so the check runs there: `agent-secrets NAME -- sh -c 'echo "${#NAME}"; case "$NAME" in ghp_*) echo prefix-ok;; *) echo prefix-no;; esac'`.
