# Go daemon workflow

This Go daemon advances phases and starts every phase worker itself. Do not choose or start the next phase. When testing is complete, end this phase with the `legion` tool: `op: "handoff_complete"`, a `summary`, and `verdict: "pass"` or `verdict: "fail"`; the verdict lets the daemon choose the next transition.

Where this project opens a production check — `production_check` in its `legion.yaml`, which names the tester unless it is `off` — that check is your work after the close. At the merge the daemon hands this session a task that names no phase and whose first line is `Production check of <KEY>: <title>.`, naming the repository, the pull request, the merge commit, the look's number, what woke it (`the merge` for the first look; `the wake named at the last look — <reason>` for a look your own `wake_at` started, the reason being the summary you sent, or for the retry of a look nobody reported, the reason being that retry's sentence; `the bound: production did not carry the merge within <N days>` for the bound's look) and whether it is the last. A session resumed on it tells it from your last phase task by that line. Do what the deployment instructions say to reach production, read whether production carries the merge, then report with `handoff_complete` and a `verdict`, committing nothing and writing no handoff: `pass` once you drove the changed path in production and recorded the result against the issue's `after_merge` check with `dispatch result` at the merge commit, naming what woke this look, with the line it prints in the pull request body; `pending` when production does not carry the merge yet, the summary naming what is missing and what you will look for next, nothing recorded and that line left at `- Check <block id> (after_merge): not yet run`; `fail` for a defect, recorded as a failing result, never an issue you file. Send a defect with a clear fix to the controller topic your `Legion addressing` line names, with its evidence comment on the controller's report issue. Open a `dispatch ask` on that report issue for a defect that needs a decision, then publish its reference to that topic. Either names this issue, the pull request, the merge commit, what you drove and what failed. The result and the summary name the message or the ask.

A `pending` report also names `wake_at`, an RFC 3339 time: when you want your next look. Nothing else starts one. A time less than a minute ahead is moved out to a minute, and a time at or after the check's own bound starts no look of its own, since the bound's last look already stands. At that report the daemon ends your process, keeps your session and releases the issue's pod; the next look brings the pod back and resumes this session with a new task that names the summary you sent as what woke it. So wait for nothing in this session: end the turn with the report.

Each look is bounded at 2 hours, and the check at the project's `production_check_hours` from the close. A look nobody reports — its 2 hours, an operator's hold, or a start that failed — is tried again two hours later, and on the last look it closes the check as not run. The task of the bound's look says it is the last: that look's `pending` carries no `wake_at` and closes the check as not run, so before reporting it write on the pull request that the check did not run because production did not carry the merge within the bound. The daemon refuses a report on an issue with no open check (`CHECK_NOT_OPEN`), on a look already ended (`CHECK_LOOK_NOT_OPEN`), from a role the check does not record (`CHECK_ROLE_MISMATCH`), one without a verdict of pass, pending or fail (`CHECK_VERDICT_REQUIRED`), a `pending` on a look that is not the last carrying no `wake_at` (`CHECK_WAKE_REQUIRED`, which records nothing and leaves the look open, so the same report with a time is taken), and a `wake_at` no look follows — on a `pass`, on a `fail`, or on a `pending` on the last look (`CHECK_WAKE_UNEXPECTED`). One refusal is about the turn rather than the report: a completion sent from a turn that is not a look's own task is refused `CHECK_LOOK_NOT_SERVED`, which records nothing — report again in the turn of the look's task, which names the look and asks for the verdict. The record lives on the pull request and in that result: a closed issue takes a result for an `after_merge` or `default_branch` check and refuses every other write. `skill://legion-worker/references/merge-gate.md` has the whole of it.

A verdict that must carry the daemon's own numbers — what a deployment's idle release has released, resumed and held — reads them with the `legion` tool's `read_release_records`, whose one argument `since` is the RFC 3339 instant your window starts at. What the answer carries is in the tool's own description. It reads them with this session's own claim, so no credential is yours to handle and none is on disk.
