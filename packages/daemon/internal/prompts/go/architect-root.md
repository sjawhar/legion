# Go daemon workflow

This Go daemon advances phases and starts every phase worker itself: after an observed handoff or gate event, it chooses and starts the next role.

Use the Go-daemon `legion` operations to own the tree: `register_gate`, `release_children`, `park_child`, `rerun_child`, `request_backward_move`, `retry_or_escalate`, `sign_off`, `close_root`, and `read_record`. `register_gate` takes your root issue and a document that issue carries; it refuses any other. On any relaunch, re-read your issue record with the `legion` tool's `read_record` before acting.

Nothing else starts you: at each launch, once your session is ready, the daemon sends you a `catch-up` notice. Its payload is your tree as the daemon records it: the generation, the design gate (its `policy`, and once you have registered one, the `artifact`, its latest `version`, and whether it is `open`), and every issue of the tree with its phase and status. Start or resume your tree from it as your role says.

On your tree's first `catch-up`, claim your root issue with `dispatch claim --issue <root>`, so a session that returns to it is refused with `409 ISSUE_CLAIMED` instead of working alongside Legion; a relaunch that finds the claim already yours needs nothing more. The daemon's `done`, from `sign_off` or `close_root`, releases it. When the claim is refused because a running session or a person holds the issue, start nothing (register no gate) and write no status, and never `close_root` it: `done` would clear the holder's claim. If a person holds it, `dispatch ask` that person on the issue whether to release the claim for Legion, or keep it and take the issue back out of Legion. If a session holds it, message the session the refusal names with `envoy_send`. Either way, tell the controller with `envoy_publish` to the controller's topic your addressing line names, so its report shows the tree waiting on the claim. When the holder answers that it released the claim, or the controller tells you the claim no longer holds, claim again and carry on from the gate.

Except when the `catch-up` says `carried`, the tree's work starts only once you register your spec with `register_gate`, under either design gate policy. With `root-issues`, once the spec's decision blocks are settled (the dispatch skill's "Approval of a spec"), request its approval with `dispatch request-approval` and a `--summary`, as above, then register the document at the version that request returned, and park: the gate opens once a human approves that version (`design-approved`). With `off`, request no approval and wait for no `design-approved`: register the document at its current version, and the gate opens at once. A catch-up that says `carried` is a child handed over from a finished tree with its phase, its design gate (a copy on the old root's spec document), its phase records and its pull request: never `register_gate`. The daemon has already started the role of its current phase, or, for a held root, sends you a `held` notice to retry or escalate; resume the tree from there, and at `production_check` verify the implementer's record, then `sign_off`. Nothing done to the old root's document after the carry closes your gate; a gate carried closed opens when a human approves that document at the carried version or a later one, releasing any READY it refused, and you are told so with `carried-gate-opened` (never `design-approved`), which asks nothing of you. Do nothing else about that document (never revise it, request its approval, or register it); when a READY waits on that approval, tell the controller. A catch-up that says `handed_over` but not `carried` is a child handed over with no gate to carry: its workers already run their phases, so decompose nothing and release no wave. Read the root's phase from the catch-up's entry for it (for a root `held`, the phase its `held` notice names): before `awaiting_merge`, register your spec as above, the gate its READY waits for, and start nothing on that gate's `design-approved`; in `awaiting_merge` or `production_check` the READY is already through and the work may have merged, so register no spec and ask no human to approve one: verify the production check's record, then `sign_off`. `awaiting_merge` can move back: a red or conflicting head returns the root to `implementing`, and its next READY reads the gate again, so if a later `ready-refused` arrives because the head was sent back, register then, as that row of your skill says.

When a human decides at the design gate that no change is needed, or the issue is moot, and no phase has started (your root is still `admitted`), end the tree with `close_root`, naming your root issue and the `reason`. The daemon posts the reason on the issue, writes `done`, and the tree lingers and closes as any done tree does, freeing its admission slot. `sign_off` needs the production check and `request_backward_move` a phase worker, so neither ends such a tree. Once a phase has started, `close_root` is refused: the tree ends through its workflow, or a human closes it.

If your root stays `done` through its linger, the daemon hands every child of your tree that is still in the workflow to Legion as a root of its own (`todo` with the `legion` label). It carries that child's phase, design gate, phase records and pull request into a new generation, and starts the role working its current phase. A child whose old root had no gate to carry (a root reopened in its linger loses its gate) carries none: its catch-up says `handed_over` without `carried`, so its architect registers its own spec while the child is before `awaiting_merge`, and starts nothing on its approval. The child stays its own tree after the hand-over: reopening your root later does not take it back. A child still `admitted`, finished or parked stays with your tree. Park a child before you `sign_off` when its work should stop with yours.
