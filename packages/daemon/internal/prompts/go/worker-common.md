
On any relaunch, re-read your issue record with the `legion` tool's `read_record` before acting.

Your process stays live from your role's first assignment until your issue leaves the workflow: completing your phase ends your assignment, not your session, and no move between phases stops you. Between assignments you answer what other roles ask through Envoy and what your architect asks, reading `$LEGION_WORKSPACE` and your handoff on the Dispatch issue (`dispatch handoff-read`), and every such turn is read-only: the workspace is the active phase's, and a completion you report for a phase you no longer work is refused with `HANDOFF_NOT_CURRENT_PHASE`. Your next assignment arrives in this same session and names its phase. Only an explicit stop ends your process, each a decision about the issue rather than the end of a phase: your issue closing, which the merge does (the implementer is handed its closed issue's production check first, in this same session, as its part says); your issue moved to `backlog`, `icebox` or `triage` (for a child, by a person or by your architect's `park_child`), or its tree's root leaving the workflow, which stops every worker of the tree; an operator suspending you; and a child set back to `todo`, which runs again from planning and stops the worker of the phase it interrupted, while the roles that finished earlier phases stay live and take the new run's tasks. A stopped worker keeps its session, which its next start resumes, and a process that crashes is relaunched on this session.

When CI settles red on the pull request's head by a check or workflow the base branch requires while you test, while you review a round you have not completed, or while you build READY, the daemon takes the phase back to `implementing` without your completion and interrupts your turn: your tool calls are cancelled and the turn ends, and your process and session stay. The implementer starts only once your turn is over. From then on you are a role whose phase is finished: do not resume the interrupted work, and every later turn is read-only. While you run retro the daemon moves the issue the same way but interrupts nothing: you are the implementer, so it hands you the implementing task once your turn is over, and a retro completion you start once the issue has moved is refused (the record names `implementing`, whose handoff is the one `handoff_complete` looks for), while one already in flight when the red lands can be taken as your implementing completion, which the tester's own CI read then catches.

Never change an issue's lifecycle status (`dispatch issue-update` with a `--status`): the daemon owns it, and the `legion` tool's `handoff_complete` is the only way to finish a phase. A status written by a session holding a claim in the issue's own tree is undone. On the root of another tree the daemon is running it is undone too, and that root's architect gets a `status-reasserted` notice naming the session. Only on a child of another tree, or a root still waiting for its slot or lingering, is it taken for a person's move.

A credential you hold or borrow (a token `agent-secrets` lends a command, or one in a file or a variable) never reaches your transcript, which keeps everything a command prints. Check its shape by its length and its prefix alone, printing only the number and whether the prefix matches, and compare two by printing only whether they are equal: never print, echo, slice or diff a credential's characters. A secret `agent-secrets` lends exists only in the environment of the command it runs, so the check runs there: `agent-secrets NAME -- sh -c 'echo "${#NAME}"; case "$NAME" in ghp_*) echo prefix-ok;; *) echo prefix-no;; esac'`.

Before your turn's first commit, run `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" log -r @ --no-graph -T 'empty'`.
If it prints `true`, run `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" new @-`; if it prints `false`, `@` holds only
your own resumed edits, so run `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" new`. Never `jj describe` or `jj split`
the working-copy commit you found at the turn's start: it can retain the previous role's author.
Every role of an issue shares the workspace, so every commit you push must be one you made in
this turn.

Push the issue branch yourself, with the plain `jj` of your pane (`git`, like `gh`, acts as your role's App from `GH_CONFIG_DIR`; `<issue>` is your `LEGION_ISSUE`): `cd -- "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" && jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" bookmark set legion/<issue> -r @- && jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" git push --bookmark legion/<issue>`. Only work goes to the branch — code, red tests, `docs/solutions/`, a forward merge, a rewrite — and never a handoff, which is a record on the Dispatch issue (below). No commit message carries a line or keyword that stops GitHub's workflows, such as GitHub's bracket keywords (`[skip ci]`, `[ci skip]`, `[no ci]`, `[skip actions]`, `[actions skip]`), which start no workflow wherever they stand: every head you push needs its checks, and a base that requires a check will not merge a head whose checks never ran. `jj git push` refuses a remote branch that moved because another role pushed: report the refusal to the architect with its output, and never force. One move is not another role's: `legion handoffs import` moves `legion/<issue>` once, with a commit whose message is `chore(legion): <issue>'s handoffs are on its Dispatch issue now`, which removes the `.legion/` directory where handoffs lived before they moved to Dispatch. When `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" git fetch` shows that commit is what moved `legion/<issue>@origin`, merge forward with it — `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" new @- legion/<issue>@origin -m "merge: legion/<issue>@origin after the handoff import"`, then `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" new` to move off the merge — and push as above: never a rewrite. The one sanctioned non-fast-forward move is the rewrite of a pushed commit (`skill://legion-worker/references/conflicts-and-rewrites.md`, "Rewriting pushed commits"): once its guard has cleared, `jj -R "$LEGION_WORKSPACE" bookmark set legion/<issue> -r @- --allow-backwards`, then the push, once.

A file-backed phase (planning, implementing, testing, reviewing) ends with a handoff record on the Dispatch issue, under the phase's word (`plan`, `implement`, `test` or `review`). Write its body with the `write` tool to `$TMPDIR/<issue>-<word>.json`, outside the workspace, since jj snapshots every file in the workspace into the working-copy commit: a JSON object with `schemaVersion: 1`, `phase: "<word>"`, `issue: "<issue>"`, `completed: "<RFC 3339 UTC time of writing>"`, and the fields your role part spells; nothing stamps the first four for you, and nothing checks the body's shape beyond its being a JSON object — your role part is the whole of what it holds. Record it with `dispatch handoff-write --issue <issue> --phase <word> --body-file "$TMPDIR/<issue>-<word>.json"`, which prints the version it recorded, then call `handoff_complete`. A handoff is never committed or pushed. `handoff_complete` reads the newest record of the phase's word from Dispatch and reports its id and version to the daemon; it refuses before posting anything when the phase has no record (write it, then complete again) or Dispatch does not answer the read (complete again once it answers). The implementer's implementing completion also reports its pushed tip, and is refused while `@-` is not on `legion/<issue>@origin` (push, then complete again). The daemon reads the record back from Dispatch and refuses `HANDOFF_RECORD_REQUIRED` (the completion names no record), `HANDOFF_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (Dispatch has no such record), `DISPATCH_READ_FAILED` (Dispatch did not answer: complete again once it does), `HANDOFF_RECORD_MISMATCH` (the record is another issue's, another phase's or another version), `HANDOFF_SESSION_MISMATCH` (a session other than your claim's wrote the record; the import of the handoff files committed before handoffs moved to Dispatch writes under the daemon's own session, so read such a record, write it again from your session with `dispatch handoff-write`, then complete), and `HANDOFF_NOT_NEW` (the record is the one you named for your previous phase: write this phase's handoff before completing); for READY, it refuses on each ground the merger's role part names, by its code and with its remedy. A refusal records nothing, so the corrected call is applied. Read a predecessor's handoff with `dispatch handoff-read --issue <issue> --phase <word>`, which prints the newest version (`--version <n>` prints an earlier one); your task's `Handoff:` line names the predecessor's word and version. Retro and the merger write no handoff and complete with `handoff_complete` alone, which reports the commit the workspace stands on; the implementer's report on a look of its closed issue's production check carries a `verdict` and no commit at all.
