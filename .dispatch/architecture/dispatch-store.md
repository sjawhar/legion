The schema every other package reads and writes through: ordered migrations applied in one transaction each at boot, the connection pools, and the session and people rows — which is why it imports `auth`. `internal/pgmigrate` holds the runner's rules, which the secrets broker's runner shares: the whole migration set is refused before anything applies when two files share a version or a file is misnamed or unreadable, and every migration's lock waits are bounded at five seconds. `internal/pgmigrate` reads migration and census SQL as Postgres 18's lexer does. A process's connections are one budget of 87 (`store.ConnectionsPerProcess`), partitioned into pools sized in code so no side can starve another: requests 48, background loops 6, live-update readers 4, settlements 4, document loads 4, the health probe 1, the 3 LISTEN connections (the listener pool's one dial is one of them redialling), leases 15 (one per name on `duty`'s lease list, thirteen, and two spare), the outbox 1 and the server process registry 1 (opened and closed by `processes.Register` from `store.Pool.OpenRegistry`), each pool's connections named `dispatch-<pool>` in `application_name`. Every way a guarded pool hands out a connection refuses a second one to a caller already holding one (`ErrNestedAcquire`), its request's pinned connection included: the pool that pinned it runs its calls on that connection, and every other guarded pool refuses it. Each pool's acquires are counted by an acquire tracer and bounded at that pool's bound, past which the pool answers `ErrPoolBusy` — the guarded pools and the raw ones a caller works through as pgx pools alike (`store.RawPool`: document loads, leases, LISTEN, the registry), so no caller has to read a want of connections off a bare `context.DeadlineExceeded` — all but the outbox's, the registry's and the health probe's, which have no bound because each of those pools has one caller that holds at most its one connection (`store/acquire.go`; the probe bounds its wait, dial and query together instead). An API read runs on one pinned request-pool connection for its whole length (`PinConnection`), and a transaction commits apart from its caller's cancellation, within ten seconds; a commit the database does not answer within that bound is `ErrCommitTimeout`, whose outcome is unknown, where a busy pool's is that nothing ran. `internal/dispatch/admission` travels with the pools it is sized to: it admits each request only while the request pool has room for its class (14 read units and 6 write units per CPU the process may use, capped at 28 and 12, so 28 and 12 on two CPUs or more; a queue twice as long as the limit and at most 1 s of waiting), refuses the rest at once with `429 OVERLOADED` and `Retry-After`, and answers a busy pool or a read past its deadline with a retryable `503 DATABASE_BUSY` or `503 REQUEST_TIMEOUT`, a commit of unknown outcome with `500 COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` and a request whose client went away with nothing, logged `status=499`.

The store also holds what the related-item check reads and writes: each project's term statistics (`search_term_stats`, `search_term_totals`, rebuilt under the `search-term-stats` lease), one `related_checks` row per check, and the trigger that embeds an issue as its title and spec (migrations 0139 and 0140).
