The publisher that turns each appended event into deliveries: its owner's topic, its route and its author and follower routes. One process publishes at a time, under a hold it renews. It sends each owner's events to each destination in order, retries a failed destination with exponential backoff, and records what a batch came to in one statement. It is the one package of the server that imports the generated Go contracts, because the envelope it publishes is the contract. Each destination it reaches is appended to the event's `published_destinations`, and that record is a contract of its own now: besides the retry, which publishes only the destinations not yet recorded, `GET /api/v1/agents/{session_id}/answers` (`sessionanswers.List`) lists a person's event for a session exactly when that session's own topic is recorded there. A change to which routes reach a session, or to what is recorded, changes what every agent is shown on its next model request.

## One publisher, and take-over

Every envoy-dispatch process runs an outbox (`outbox.New`, `Run`). Only the process holding the publisher hold reads and sends events: `dispatch-outbox`, a `duty.Hold` row in `duty_holds` (migration `0148`). The hold lasts 6 s on the database's clock after each renewal, is renewed every 2 s, and reports itself lost 2 s before it lapses on the process's monotonic clock. The hold is taken and renewed on the outbox's own pool (`store.Pool.Outbox`, two connections), so a renewal never waits behind a batch statement or behind the API's requests on the shared pool. The others stand by.

- **Killed holder.** Its row lapses 6 s after its last renewal. A standby arms one timer to that moment (`duty.Expiry` reads what is left on the database's clock), takes the hold when the timer fires, and scans at once.
- **Stopping holder.** It takes no further batch. In the batch in flight it makes no further send once its context ends, settles the sends still in flight as lost, records what NATS already answered and releases the rest, and then releases the hold. The release deletes the row and sends `pg_notify('dispatch_duty_holds', 'dispatch-outbox')` in one statement, which wakes a standby at once. A graceful stop of `envoy-dispatch` waits for both before it closes the database pool, for at most `outboxStopBudget` after its shutdown starts (`cmd/dispatch/shutdown.go`).
- **Holder without NATS.** It releases the hold when its client has been disconnected for 3 s without a break and still reads disconnected when the 3 s end: the grace timer reads `Connected()` again, because a reconnect's event can wait in nats.go's callback queue behind a slow callback, and a client connected again keeps the hold and scans. It also releases the hold when a batch's sends fail for the connection (`bus.ErrUnavailable`, or `bus.ErrNotAcknowledged` on a connection still current) and a 2 s flush probe of that window's connection fails too. That probe is what finds a silent cut, a connection that still reports itself connected. A client that has already reconnected since the batch began keeps the hold and resends on the new connection. A send that waits for a connection reads it again at each connection event and at least every second (`bus.reconnectRecheck`), for the same queued event.
- **Standby.** It tries the hold only while its own NATS client is connected. A process that gave the hold up for NATS loss also first needs an answered probe of its connection before it tries again, so it does not take back the hold it gave up for that same silent connection ahead of a standby whose NATS works. If both processes lose NATS, neither holds until one reconnects.

`/healthz` reports `outbox_publisher`: `true` while this process holds the hold and its loss deadline has not passed, and `false` on a standby, after a loss, and with NATS disabled. It never decides `ok`.

## Fencing

A process publishes only while it holds the hold and its loss deadline has not passed. A batch sends through a window that carries the deadline (`bus.Client.WindowUntil`), and its record checks the deadline too. The window checks it before anything else of each send, caps a send's wait for a connection at it, and checks it again as the last act before the write: a send at or past the deadline writes nothing and answers `bus.ErrWindowDeadline`, which the batch reads as fenced. So a process frozen past its deadline (SIGSTOP, a long pause) sends nothing and writes nothing when it resumes, and its batch is left unrecorded for the next holder. The deadline is Margin ahead of the row's lapse, so the row is still this holder's whenever the check passes. The record writes what is true whoever holds the hold, regardless of the hold: destinations reached, and `published_at` once every destination is reached. It writes back-offs, hold-backs and releases only while the hold row still names this holder and has not lapsed, checked in the same statement. A record that finds the hold another's ends the holder as one that lost the hold. A lost hold is released like any other: the release stops its renewal, and its delete and notify are guarded on this holder, so another's row is left alone. A copy an old holder sent before it stopped lands as a duplicate, never ahead of a later event of its destination, because the new holder sends that event again first.

## Wake

Nothing scans on a period. The holder and the standby have one one-slot wake channel each. The holder's is signalled by:

- a LISTEN connection (`pglisten`, dialled through `store.Pool.Listener`) on `dispatch_events`, which migration `0107` notifies once per statement that inserts events. Every new connection signals too, to catch up on what it missed while it had none;
- the in-process events broker, so this process's own commits are seen while the LISTEN connection redials;
- every change of the NATS connection (`bus.Client.ConnectionChanged`), so a holder resends what a lost connection released;
- the holder's one retry timer, armed after each scan to the earliest due `next_attempt_at`, read as a duration on the database's clock. After a scan that failed it is armed to `retry.BaseDelay`.

The standby's is signalled only by the same LISTEN connection's `dispatch_duty_holds` notification of this hold's release, by every new LISTEN connection, and by every change of the NATS connection. The events the holder writes never wake a standby, so a standby sends no statement per event (`TestAStandbyDoesNotTryTheHoldForEveryEventTheHolderWrites`).

The holder scans on every wake. A standby only tries the hold: on its wake, on its timer to the current hold's lapse, or a second later when the database or its NATS probe did not answer. A scan takes one batch after another while the last one published anything or was full. It stops sooner after a batch that released an event or failed to read a row, after a statement that failed (the batch's read, a lookup other than a recipient read, or its record), and when the hold's deadline passed, a record found the hold another's, or NATS was lost.

## The batch read

`readBatch` writes no row: with one publisher, no row needs a claim. In one statement it chooses up to 100 owners that have a due event: the lowest-id events never tried (`events_outbox_fresh`) and the earliest back-offs that have fallen due (`events_outbox_retry`). It then reads each chosen owner's unpublished events in id order, due or not, up to 100 per owner (`events_outbox_owner`, on `coalesce(issue_key, artifact_id::text, project_key)`). The batch keeps at most 100 due events. An owner is an issue, a document or a project. All three indexes are partial (migration `0149`) and exclude published rows and pending subscription commands. So a read never walks past backed-off rows of owners the batch does not choose, or past commands that are never published. An event with no owner (session-addressed, seq 0) has no destination and is marked published.

The followers, authors, recipients and mention deliveries a batch routes by are read once per batch: one set-based statement per kind (ask followers, thread root authors, message authors, document credits, the `comment_deliveries` of every new comment whose mentions resolved to a session, the sessions a person's reply reaches in its thread, and the sessions of the primary document of an issue nobody routes or claims), so at most 7 lookup statements whatever the batch's size (`loadLookups`). An id is read in its canonical form, and one that is no uuid is never sent to Postgres: it fails the event that asked it, except a reply's `reply_to`, which then replies under no comment and so reaches no thread session, fails nothing and holds nothing back. A lookup statement that fails fails the batch, which is read again, except a recipient read (document credits, thread sessions, primary document sessions). A recipient statement Postgres refuses over the rows it read (`refusedStatement`) is run again in halves, and each refused half in halves, until each refused row is run alone (`loadRecipients`): an id in a run that answers gets its sessions, and only the events that asked an id whose own run is refused fail, whatever owners asked the statement's other ids. One broken row among n ids costs at most 2*ceil(log2(n)) more statements. A recipient statement that fails for the process's stop or for the connection (lost, refused at connect, or a server error of SQLSTATE class 08, 40, 53, 57 or 58, such as the `admin_shutdown` of a failover) is not run again and fails every event that asked it. A failed event's destinations end at the step that needed the read, after the owner topic, the route and the claimant, and it is backed off, or released when the process's stop failed the read. An event that is not due reads its recipients as a due one does, since they are what it holds its owner's later events back from. The issue's route and the session holding its claim (the claimant route, `addClaimantRoute`) are columns of the batch's read itself (`routing`), read again by every batch, so they cost no lookup.

A new comment's mention deliveries decide its session copies (`destinations.leftToMention`): a session a mention reached is skipped, one whose mention is still being sent (a `pending` attempt younger than `model.DeliveryClaimLapse`) has its copy held back, and every other is sent to as if it had not been mentioned. A held-back copy is no destination of its event, so it holds none of the owner's later events back.

## Order

Order is kept per (owner, destination key). A destination key is the subject family a session receives on: the owner's topic family under `notifications.dispatch.` is one, and each `notifications.agent.<session>` subject is one, whichever route reaches it. A role route's key is its holder's agent subject. `planChains` builds one chain per owner and key, in id order. `sendChains` sends every chain through one window, and sends a chain's next envelope only once the answer to the one before it came back as reached. So no two sends of one (owner, destination key) are ever in flight, and different owners and keys proceed side by side.

A destination that stalls (a send that failed, or an event waiting out a back-off with that destination unreached) holds back that destination's later events of that owner and nothing else; the one wider hold is an event whose recipient read failed, below. Such a held-back event is recorded `blocked`: it falls due when the earliest of its blockers does, with no attempt counted, and is read again beside its blocker. A blocker of the same batch that was released releases the event it held back too. An event that cannot be read at all (a scan or decode failure) is backed off and holds nothing back. It is the only event that stops blocking without being published.

An event whose recipient read failed has a list that ends at that read, so it may still be bound for any session the read would name. Every later event of its owner is held back from each session key (an agent subject, or a role route's key) that the failed event neither names in its list nor already reached (`published_destinations`), behind a blocker that falls due when it is tried again: its back-off or release when it was due, its `next_attempt_at` when it was not. A key its list names chains as usual, a key it already reached holds nothing back, and the owner's topic family is never held this way. So a row no recipient read can take, such as a credit list `jsonb_array_elements` refuses, backs off the events that read it and holds back their owners' later session copies on the keys those events could still owe, until their retry, and nothing else. An event whose reads all answer, due or not, holds back only the destinations it has not reached.

Residual: a session that subscribes to an issue's topic family and is also routed to by its events receives on two keys. If event N's topic publish fails while N+1's agent publish succeeds, that session sees N+1's first copy before N's. Both are JetStream publishes on the same stream, so this takes a stream failing one subject and not the other.

## Role routes

A `role:<id>` route is not published to `notifications.role.<id>`: nothing of Dispatch's crosses `notifications.role.*` any more. The outbox resolves the role's holder itself with `roles.ResolveHolder`, the resolution the listener's role lane also uses, so the two cannot drift. It resolves once per role per batch, reading the role and session buckets directly (`roles.Buckets` on the outbox's bus client). The outcomes:

- no claim: the destination counts as reached, nothing sent;
- a claim whose holder's session is gone: the claim is released (`ReleaseExpiredRoleClaim`, a compare and set). A release that finds a fresh claim in its place resolves the role once more, two reads of the claim in all (`roles.resolutionAttempts`). Once released, or found already gone, the destination is reached with nothing sent. A claim the store kept, or one replaced again by the second read, stays pending;
- a read that failed, or a holder that has not heartbeated within `session.ClaimStaleAfter`: pending, backed off with an attempt counted and retried;
- the holder is the event's own actor session (the listener's echo skip): reached, nothing sent;
- otherwise a forward to `notifications.agent.<holder>` with a window `Request`. It carries the envelope a listener forward carries: topic `notifications.role.<id>`, dedupe key `envoy.role.forward.` plus the event's key (`contracts.RoleForwardDedupePrefix`), event id unchanged. It is sent under a `Nats-Msg-Id` of that key and subject (`bus.ForwardMsgID`), so the stream stores one copy however often it is sent. The listener's own role forward (`roleTopicDelivery`, `cmd/listener/delivery.go`) passes `bus.Client.RequestCoreTo` that MsgId only when the original envelope's key names its event (`contracts.DedupeKeyNamesTheUpstreamEvent`, asked of the envelope before the prefix is added), which every Dispatch envelope's does.

The forward's receipt decides it, within 5 s. A self-subscribed holder answers with an empty receipt. A holder with a serving port never answers the request itself, so the stream's acknowledgement of the stored copy counts as its receipt. That holds once the listener delivers to such holders from the stream and acknowledges each stored copy; until then its durable acknowledges a stored role forward without delivering it (`cmd/listener/delivery.go`, `roleTopicDelivery`). No receipt leaves the destination pending and retried. The host plugins apply their receipt and dedupe to the forward unchanged.

## Failure classes

Once every answer of a batch is in:

- **released**, with no attempt counted and due again at once: a send that met a lost or replaced connection (`bus.ErrUnavailable`) or the process stopping. Released events are sent again on the reconnect's wake. When the batch's connection was replaced since it began, or its probe failed (NATS lost, above), the not-acknowledged sends are released too. A batch in which a send met the hold's deadline, or whose deadline passed before its record, is left unrecorded instead, not released (Fencing, above);
- **backed off**, with the attempt counted and the error recorded in `events.publish_error`: a send NATS denied (`bus.ErrPublishDenied`) or did not acknowledge on a connection still current (`bus.ErrNotAcknowledged`: no acknowledgement in time, no responders, no receipt from a role's holder), a topic the stream does not capture (`bus.ErrNotCaptured`), a role route left pending, or an event whose destinations could not all be resolved. The delay is `retry.Delay` of the attempt count, capped at 5 min, added to the database's clock (`now() + make_interval(...)`);
- **blocked**, with no attempt counted: besides an event an earlier event holds back (Order, above), an event that held a session's copy back for a mention still being sent. Such an event falls due `mentionPendingDelay` (2 s) later at the latest, when its mention deliveries are read again, or sooner when an earlier event holding it back falls due sooner. A held-back copy blocks the event only when no send of it failed or was lost: an event that also failed or met a lost connection is backed off or released as it would be without the mention;
- **reached**: a send NATS refuses the same way however often it is sent (`bus.ErrRefused`: past the max payload, or a subject NATS would not take). It is logged once (`dispatch outbox: destination refused`) and the event goes on to its other destinations.

## The record

`recordBatch` writes a batch's outcomes in one statement, so all of it or none, and an event row is written once per attempt. It appends each reached destination to `published_destinations`. It sets `published_at` and clears `publish_error` once every destination is reached. Behind the hold check of "Fencing" above, it writes back-off (`next_attempt_at`, `attempt_count`, `publish_error`), hold-back (`next_attempt_at` alone: the earlier of its delay out, which a blocker's back-off or a mention still being sent gives it, and a not-due blocker's due time) and release (`next_attempt_at = null`). Every row is guarded on `published_at is null`. A record that fails leaves the batch as it was read, as a process that dies between NATS's acknowledgements and the record does. The next scan or the next holder sends it again: JetStream drops a repeat by MsgId inside its 72-hour duplicate window, and a role's holder drops a repeat forward by its dedupe key. Delivery is therefore at least once, and each receiver takes each event once.
