The shapes Dispatch's background loops run in, and the lease that keeps two processes from doing one loop's work at once. `RunWoken` is the change-driven loop: one pass at start, then a pass only when its wake is signalled or a due time its last pass returned comes round, never sooner than its least gap after the last pass started, so an idle database costs it nothing. `RunJittered` is the timer loop for work GitHub gives no change to wake on: a random first delay within one interval, then the interval plus up to a tenth again, drawn afresh every period. `Exclusive` is the lease either may run a pass under: a session-level two-key advisory lock on a connection of its own, and, for a loop with a period, a record of the period's pass in `duty_passes`, so a timer loop runs once per period across the processes; a woken loop takes it with no period and retries a held lease until a pass of its own runs. `Hold` is the other shape, for work one process does for as long as it can rather than one pass at a time: a named row in `duty_holds` taken and renewed on the holder's own pool, lapsing on the database's clock so no two hosts' wall clocks are compared, reported lost to its holder before it lapses, and released by a delete that notifies `dispatch_duty_holds` in the same statement, which wakes a standby at once. The events outbox's publisher holds `dispatch-outbox`. It imports no store package — a caller hands it the lease pool — so the edges to `dispatch-store` are the `duty_passes` and `duty_holds` tables, which store migrations create.
