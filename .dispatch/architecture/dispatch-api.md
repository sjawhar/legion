Every HTTP route body: issues, artifacts, asks, comments, messages, projects, search, and the assignee, rank, label and component-attachment writes, and the per-session list of what people wrote to an agent (`GET /api/v1/agents/{session_id}/answers`, `session_answers.go`), which every agent host reads on each model request. Search reads each ask hit's answer state from its row, and a resolved ask's last answer from its `ask.answered` events, before it answers. The per-session list's read model is the small package `sessionanswers` (`List`), which travels with its one route: a person's events whose `published_destinations` hold the session's own topic (`contracts.AgentSubject`), its sent message and comment deliveries, each item at its current words as a `model.SessionAnswer` with its relation to the session and its `dispatch://` and deep-link place; the outbox's tests read back through it, so the route reads what the publisher records. The largest package in the server (87 files) and the one every Dispatch feature touches. It resolves an agent's actor through the listener's OIDC and routing packages, hence the edge to `envoy-listener`. The edge to `contracts` is the wire contract rather than a Go import: `contracts/src/dispatch-api.ts` declares the response shapes these handlers produce and the web app consumes, and code generation covers only the event envelope, so there is no Go type to import. It also runs the related-item check every issue create, ask and new decision block gets once its write commits (a search by the text's rarest words and by meaning, waited on for at most 5 s and recorded in `related_checks`), the no-write route and the records route over it, and the term-statistics refresh its keyword picker reads.

The live paths hold bounded state per reader. The event stream relay (`event_stream_relay.go`) is the process's one reader of the event log: its first subscriber starts a generation that listens on `dispatch_events` and reads each commit once, encoding each event's frame once for every subscriber, and the cancel of its last subscriber stops it at once, so a process nobody subscribes to holds no LISTEN connection and reads nothing. The events view of `GET /ws/live` (`live_events.go`) subscribes to it. A resume at or below the relay's head is replayed at most 256 events whose frames total at most 1 MiB, counted by rows; past either bound it is replayed nothing. Its `events_start` says `replay_complete: true` only when the replay is everything the page lacks, which includes a resume ahead of this process's relay but within the log; a page whose subscription starts without it refreshes what it shows. A subscription whose 64-slot relay subscription overflows catches up from the log within the same bounds and goes on; past them, or when the relay's generation ends (an event log reset, shutdown), it ends as `behind` and the page subscribes again. The delivery hub takes its events from the relay's feed (1,024 slots) and itself listens only on `delivery_changes` and `delivery_freshness`; it also stops with its last view, a closed feed makes it reload the open P0 issues and recompute every view, and an event that arrives during a view's read and touches the answer that read gives recomputes that view again. A comment's mentions are resolved in its request, up to 8 lookups at once, each bounded at 250 ms, so a listener that answers none delays a comment with up to 8 mentions by one 250 ms timeout and each further 8 mentions add one (ceil(k/8) timeouts for k mentions); the attempts commit pending with the comment, and the response returns them pending. The mention sender (`comment_mention_sender.go`) sends them after the response on one FIFO lane per recipient (the pinned session, else the target as written: `role:X` shares its holder's lane only when its lookup found the holder, and an unanswered `role:X` is keyed by its string, so two comments to one recipient can then arrive in either order), 8 lanes at once and at most 4,096 sends queued; once shutdown starts it queues nothing more (a refused send is logged at WARN), starts no queued send and gives the sends in flight 10 s. An attempt no sender finishes is the delivery sweep's (`delivery_sweep.go`, `resumeOwedDeliveries`): one process's maintenance loop, under the sweep's lease, sends once a minute every pending attempt that is the latest of its target, is claimed by nobody or by a claim that lapsed, and was created between a claim lease (one minute) and the duplicate window ago (`model.OwedMentionAttemptSQL`), whichever process left it, through the same claim; an attempt younger than the lease is left to the process that queued it, so the sweep never takes a live process's queue out of order. So the attempts a stopping process refused, left queued or could not queue for the bound are sent by a later pass once they have waited a lease (in a rolling update the successor is up before the old process stops), and a killed process's send in flight by the first pass after its claim lapses, within a minute and a sweep interval of its taking, under its own idempotency key, so a send that had landed is dropped as a repeat. Each pass reads the partial indexes `message_deliveries_pending` and `comment_deliveries_pending` (pending rows by `created_at`): with nothing owed it is one statement a minute that reads no row; no event can drive it, since a killed process sends no signal and a stopping one refuses its sends after its lifetime ends. Order holds within one process only: an attempt resumed after a crash or a handoff can arrive after later comments. A session a mention may reach also gets the new comment's own copy on the route, the claimant, an author or a follower route only by one rule the outbox applies when it publishes (`attempt.leftToMention` in `internal/dispatch/outbox`), keyed by the one session each attempt counts under (`model.MentionSession`: an unanswered `session:<id>` mention counts as `<id>`'s, and a route equal to an unanswered mention's target follows that target's attempts). What the attempt came to - sent, live, abandoned, or owed to the sweep, each a predicate over its row in `internal/dispatch/model`, all built from the sweep's own definition - decides the copy and which of the outbox, a sender's claim and the sweep acts on the attempt; a copy held back for a resumed mention is a `comment_fallback_copies` row, which the outbox's fallback pass publishes to its one destination only once the resend fails, never touching the event's own row. `publishFallbackCopies` in `internal/dispatch/outbox/mentions.go` is the protocol's one description. So a mentioned session loses no comment through a kill. The copy on the own topic of a session with a failed (or abandoned) attempt, and every fallback copy, carries that attempt's mention dedupe key, `agent.<session>.<comment>:<target>:<mode>` (`contracts.AgentSendDedupeKey` of `model.CommentMentionIdempotencyKey`, the lowest failed target's when there are several), in place of `dispatch-<event id>`, which every other destination carries and which stays its event id. So a send the killed process had handed the listener before it died, whose resend then fails for good (the listener down after the restart), is not shown twice: the host drops a frame whose named dedupe key it claimed within 72 hours, and the stream, whose MsgId is the dedupe key and the subject, also drops a second message with that key on that subject within its 72-hour duplicate window. A session no mention reached never claimed the key and is shown the copy. An unanswered `role:X` mention reached under any other string, and a `role:X` route whose own lookup went unanswered, name no session to compare, so that session can get both copies. An agent view (`live_agent.go`, `agent_stream.go`) queues at most 8 MiB of frames; past that it ends as `behind`. A page that stops reading meets the socket's own bounds first (#2449): the writer's 10 s write deadline (`liveSocketWriteWait`) ends the socket, which holds at most 16 of the hub's frames (`liveSocketQueue`) and 64 stream frames (`liveSocketStream`); the agent view's 8 MiB bounds only what it buffers while the socket writes slower than the session produces.
