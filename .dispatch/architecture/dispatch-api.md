Every HTTP route body: issues, artifacts, asks, comments, messages, projects, search, and the assignee, rank, label and component-attachment writes. Search reads each ask hit's answer state from its row, and a resolved ask's last answer from its `ask.answered` events, before it answers. The largest package in the server (87 files) and the one every Dispatch feature touches. It resolves an agent's actor through the listener's OIDC and routing packages, hence the edge to `envoy-listener`. The edge to `contracts` is the wire contract rather than a Go import: `contracts/src/dispatch-api.ts` declares the response shapes these handlers produce and the web app consumes, and code generation covers only the event envelope, so there is no Go type to import.

The live paths hold bounded state per reader. The event stream relay (`event_stream_relay.go`) is the process's one reader of the event log: its first subscriber starts a generation that listens on `dispatch_events` and reads each commit once, encoding each event's SSE frame once for every subscriber, and the cancel of its last subscriber stops it at once, so a process nobody subscribes to holds no LISTEN connection and reads nothing. `GET /api/v1/events` subscribes to it. A resume (`since=` or `Last-Event-ID`) at or below the relay's head is replayed at most 256 events whose frames total at most 1 MiB, counted by rows; past either bound it is replayed nothing. `X-Dispatch-Event-Replay: complete` is set only when the replay is everything the client lacks, which includes a resume ahead of this process's relay but within the log; a client that reconnects without it refetches what it shows. A connection whose 64-slot subscription overflows catches up from the log within the same bounds and stays open; past them, or when the relay's generation ends (an event log reset, the test hook, shutdown), its response ends. The delivery hub takes its events from the relay's feed (1,024 slots) and itself listens only on `delivery_changes` and `delivery_freshness`; it also stops with its last view, a closed feed makes it reload the open P0 issues and recompute every view, and an event that arrives during a view's read and touches the answer that read gives recomputes that view again. A comment's mentions are resolved in its request, up to 8 lookups at once, each bounded at 250 ms, so a listener that answers none delays a comment with up to 8 mentions by one 250 ms timeout and each further 8 mentions add one (ceil(k/8) timeouts for k mentions); the attempts commit pending with the comment, and the response returns them pending. The mention sender (`comment_mention_sender.go`) sends them after the response on one FIFO lane per recipient (the pinned session, else the target as written: `role:X` shares its holder's lane only when its lookup found the holder, and an unanswered `role:X` is keyed by its string, so two comments to one recipient can then arrive in either order), 8 lanes at once and at most 4,096 sends queued; once shutdown starts it queues nothing more (a refused send is logged at WARN), starts no queued send and gives the sends in flight 10 s. Every process runs a stranded pass at startup and every 30 s after (half the claim lease): it resumes, through the claim, pending attempts created between a claim lease (one minute) and an hour ago whose claim is unset or lapsed, from any process, except those its own sender has queued and not finished; an attempt younger than the lease is left to the process that queued it, so a pass never takes a live process's queue out of order. So the attempts a stopping process refused, left queued or could not queue for the bound are sent by a later pass once they have waited a lease (in a rolling update the successor is up before the old process stops), and a killed process's send in flight by the first pass after its claim lapses, within a minute and 30 s of its taking, under its own idempotency key, so a send that had landed is dropped as a repeat. Each pass is one read of the pending `comment_deliveries` rows per process every 30 s, and with no index on `state` that read scans the table. Order holds within one process only: an attempt resumed after a crash or a handoff can arrive after later comments. An agent-stream viewer (`agent_stream.go`) queues at most 8 MiB of frames; past that its stream ends.
