Every HTTP route body: issues, artifacts, asks, comments, messages, projects, search, and the assignee, rank, label and component-attachment writes. Search reads each ask hit's answer state from its row, and a resolved ask's last answer from its `ask.answered` events, before it answers. The largest package in the server (87 files) and the one every Dispatch feature touches. It resolves an agent's actor through the listener's OIDC and routing packages, hence the edge to `envoy-listener`. The edge to `contracts` is the wire contract rather than a Go import: `contracts/src/dispatch-api.ts` declares the response shapes these handlers produce and the web app consumes, and code generation covers only the event envelope, so there is no Go type to import.

The live paths hold bounded state per reader. The event stream relay (`event_stream_relay.go`) is the process's one reader of the event log: its first subscriber starts a generation that listens on `dispatch_events` and reads each commit once, encoding each event's SSE frame once for every subscriber, and the cancel of its last subscriber stops it at once, so a process nobody subscribes to holds no LISTEN connection and reads nothing. `GET /api/v1/events` subscribes to it. A resume (`since=` or `Last-Event-ID`) at or below the relay's head is replayed at most 256 events whose frames total at most 1 MiB, counted by rows; past either bound it is replayed nothing. `X-Dispatch-Event-Replay: complete` is set only when the replay is everything the client lacks, which includes a resume ahead of this process's relay but within the log; a client that reconnects without it refetches what it shows. A connection whose 64-slot subscription overflows catches up from the log within the same bounds and stays open; past them, or when the relay's generation ends (an event log reset, the test hook, shutdown), its response ends. The delivery hub takes its events from the relay's feed (1,024 slots) and itself listens only on `delivery_changes` and `delivery_freshness`; it also stops with its last view, a closed feed makes it reload the open P0 issues and recompute every view, and an event that arrives during a view's read and touches the answer that read gives recomputes that view again. A comment's mentions are resolved in its request, up to 8 lookups at once, each bounded at 250 ms, so a listener that answers none delays a comment with up to 8 mentions by one 250 ms timeout and each further 8 mentions add one (ceil(k/8) timeouts for k mentions); the attempts commit pending with the comment, and the response returns them pending. The mention sender (`comment_mention_sender.go`) sends them after the response on one FIFO lane per recipient (the pinned session, else the target as written: `role:X` shares its holder's lane only when its lookup found the holder, and an unanswered `role:X` is keyed by its string, so two comments to one recipient can then arrive in either order), 8 lanes at once and at most 4,096 sends queued; once shutdown starts it queues nothing more (a refused send is logged at WARN), starts no queued send and gives the sends in flight 10 s. Every process runs a stranded pass at startup and every 30 s after (half the claim lease): it resumes, through the claim, pending attempts created between a claim lease (one minute) and an hour ago whose claim is unset or lapsed, from any process, except those its own sender has queued and not finished; an attempt younger than the lease is left to the process that queued it, so a pass never takes a live process's queue out of order. So the attempts a stopping process refused, left queued or could not queue for the bound are sent by a later pass once they have waited a lease (in a rolling update the successor is up before the old process stops), and a killed process's send in flight by the first pass after its claim lapses, within a minute and 30 s of its taking, under its own idempotency key, so a send that had landed is dropped as a repeat. Each pass reads the partial index `comment_deliveries_pending` (pending rows by `created_at`): with nothing stranded it is one statement per process every 30 s that reads no row; no event can drive it, since a killed process sends no signal and a stopping one refuses its sends after its lifetime ends. Order holds within one process only: an attempt resumed after a crash or a handoff can arrive after later comments. A session a mention may reach also gets the new comment's own copy on the route, the claimant, an author or a follower route only by one rule the outbox applies when it publishes (`attempt.leftToMention` in `internal/dispatch/outbox`): the copy is skipped once the mention was sent, held back while it is pending, and published once it failed or was abandoned; an unanswered `session:<id>` mention counts as `<id>`'s, and a route equal to an unanswered mention's target follows that target's attempts. An attempt no sender ever claimed, pending past the one-minute claim lease (a send queued behind its lane that long, or one a stopped process never started), is the outbox's or the stranded pass's, whichever acts first on its row: before it publishes a copy that waits on the attempt, the outbox settles it `failed` with its `comment.delivery` receipt under the comment's owner lock, and a sender's claim after that finds it settled and sends nothing, while a claim that commits first leaves it claimed and the outbox waits on the send. An attempt a sender claimed and never settled (a send in flight at a kill) is the stranded pass's alone for an hour: its resend repeats the attempt's idempotency key, so a frame that had already reached the listener is dropped there, and the outbox publishes the comment without that session's copy meanwhile, holding each copy it skipped as a `comment_fallback_copies` row (one per comment and exact destination topic, which the event's own publish then always skips). The outbox's fallback pass, at startup and on every retry tick, publishes the `comment.created` envelope to that one destination once the resend has failed, and settles the row with nothing sent once it was sent; it never writes the event's row, so its `published_at`, `published_destinations` and payload stay as its own publish left them, and a route, claimant or follower that moved since gets nothing of the old comment (`publishFallbackCopies` in `internal/dispatch/outbox/mentions.go` describes the protocol). So a mentioned session loses no comment through a kill. The copy on the own topic of a session with a failed (or abandoned) attempt, and every fallback copy, carries that attempt's mention dedupe key, `agent.<session>.<comment>:<target>:<mode>` (`contracts.AgentSendDedupeKey` of `model.CommentMentionIdempotencyKey`, the lowest failed target's when there are several), in place of `dispatch-<event id>`, which every other destination carries and which stays its event id. So a send the killed process had handed the listener before it died, whose resend then fails for good (the listener down after the restart), is not shown twice: the host drops a frame whose named dedupe key it claimed within 72 hours, and the stream, whose MsgId is the dedupe key and the subject, also drops a second message with that key on that subject within its 72-hour duplicate window. A session no mention reached never claimed the key and is shown the copy. An unanswered `role:X` mention reached under any other string, and a `role:X` route whose own lookup went unanswered, name no session to compare, so that session can get both copies. An agent-stream viewer (`agent_stream.go`) queues at most 8 MiB of frames; past that its stream ends.
