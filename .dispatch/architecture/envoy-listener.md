Ingests GitHub, Slack and Ghost Wispr webhooks, publishes ordinary notifications through JetStream, and role lanes and ad-hoc `notifications.` topics the stream does not capture through core NATS, resolves live sessions and delivers by `prompt_async`; tracks PR check settlement. The CI store's summary loop is event-driven: the store's cache marks each record that changes, the loop keeps the records waiting out their debounce, a claim or the handover grace in a queue ordered by deadline, and it runs a pass only on a one-second tick at which a record changed or fell due, so with nothing changed it does no work and a change costs the same at any cache size. The small packages that instrument that path belong to it rather than to components of their own: OpenID Connect token verification for service accounts and the authorization code flow Dispatch's sign-in runs (`oidc`), the signature and duplicate-delivery guards (`verify`, `dedupe`), `metrics`, `id`, `logging`, listener bootstrap (`config`), the `natstail` diagnostic tail, and the listener's own harnesses (`integration`, `smoke`, `testnats`). `internal/dispatch/config` sits under the Dispatch tree but belongs here: it loads the shared `envoy.json` shape — NATS URLs, the Dispatch server URL and token — that `natstail`, the Dispatch server and the Envoy plugin all read. A session's registry entry, and its row in `GET /v1/sessions` and `GET /v1/registry/<id>`, carry the `plugin` and `plugin_version` its registration sent; a session from a plugin older than those fields has neither key.

Any number of listener processes may run for one machine id (in Kubernetes, the pods of one Deployment). Every one serves `/v1`, the webhooks and the role lane's queue group; only the holder of the machine's delivery lease (`lease.listener-<machine id>` in the `envoy_listener` KV bucket, renewed every second, taken by a standby after a 5 s TTL or at once when released) receives the durable consumer `listener-<machine id>` and runs the reapers, the interest marker collector and the CI summary loop, and every other pod is a standby. The pods share the rest of their state through NATS rather than their processes: the delivery ledger (`internal/dedupe`, the `delivery.` keys of the same bucket), each session's last-seen record (`seen.`), the machine's service start that role-claim grace counts from (`service_since` in the lease record), and compare-and-set registry writes; `/healthz` answers `503 starting` until a pod serves and then `200` naming its `role`, `active` or `standby`. `packages/envoy/cmd/listener/README.md` holds the run model, the readiness contract and the two-build rules.

The interest reaper, run by the lease holder alone, deletes a dead session's interest from the `envoy_interests` bucket only 15 minutes after the row's last write: the grace window it is given, `store.InterestReapGrace` (10 minutes), plus `store.InterestRefreshAge` (5 minutes), the most a row's last write may lag the session's last heartbeat.
