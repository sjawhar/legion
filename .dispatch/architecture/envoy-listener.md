Ingests GitHub, Slack and Ghost Wispr webhooks, publishes ordinary notifications through JetStream, and role lanes and ad-hoc `notifications.` topics the stream does not capture through core NATS, resolves live sessions and delivers by `prompt_async`; tracks PR check settlement. Its publish route answers a role or ad-hoc topic message once each recipient confirmed it (the role's holder, or each self-subscribed session registered on the topic), or 504 naming who did not. The CI store's summary loop is event-driven: the store's cache marks each record that changes, the loop keeps the records waiting out their debounce, a claim or the handover grace in a queue ordered by deadline, and it runs a pass only on a one-second tick at which a record changed or fell due, so with nothing changed it does no work and a change costs the same at any cache size. The `ENVOY_NOTIFICATIONS` stream and each KV bucket hold the deployment's replica count (`ENVOY_NATS_REPLICAS`), and the stream has a byte limit (`ENVOY_STREAM_MAX_BYTES`) past which it refuses new publishes (`DiscardNew`, a retryable `bus.ErrStreamFull` the webhook answers 503) and keeps every stored message until its 72-hour age; a clustered server's duplicate acknowledgement with no sequence, for a retry of a refused message (`bus.ErrNotStored`), has the bus publish the message once more without its `Nats-Msg-Id`, which the stream stores once it has room, and is never a success. A start raises a stream or bucket found with fewer copies, and a stream with a smaller byte limit or none, never lowers either, and keeps the stream's metadata, `envoy_stream_id` included, and its other limits on every update. JetStream's stream update has no compare-and-swap, so a concurrent start with other or no settings can put back the copies, limit and metadata it read; every owning program (the listener, Dispatch's server) therefore runs a keeper that every 30 seconds raises the stream's copies and byte limit back to its settings, puts back the discard policy and `envoy_stream_id`, and raises its own buckets' copies. An operator's raise of the limit stands, and the stream holds the most copies and the largest limit any program of a deployment carries. The small packages that instrument that path belong to it rather than to components of their own: OpenID Connect token verification for service accounts and the authorization code flow Dispatch's sign-in runs (`oidc`), the signature and duplicate-delivery guards (`verify`, `dedupe`), `metrics`, `id`, `logging`, listener bootstrap (`config`), the `natstail` diagnostic tail, and the listener's own harnesses (`integration`, `smoke`, `testnats`). `internal/dispatch/config` sits under the Dispatch tree but belongs here: it loads the shared `envoy.json` shape — NATS URLs, the Dispatch server URL and token — that `natstail`, the Dispatch server and the Envoy plugin all read. A session's registry entry, and its row in `GET /v1/sessions` and `GET /v1/registry/<id>`, carry the `plugin` and `plugin_version` its registration sent; a session from a plugin older than those fields has neither key.

Any number of listener processes may run for one machine id (in Kubernetes, the pods of one Deployment). Every one serves `/v1`, the webhooks and the role lane's queue group; only the holder of the machine's delivery lease (`lease.listener-<machine id>` in the `envoy_listener` KV bucket, renewed every second, taken by a standby after a 5 s TTL or at once when released) receives the durable consumer `listener-<machine id>` and runs the reapers, the interest marker collector and the CI summary loop, and every other pod is a standby. The pods share the rest of their state through NATS rather than their processes: the delivery ledger (`internal/dedupe`, the `delivery.` keys of the same bucket), each session's last-seen record (`seen.`), the machine's service start that role-claim grace counts from (`service_since` in the lease record), and compare-and-set registry writes; `/healthz` answers `503 starting` until a pod serves and then `200` naming its `role`, `active` or `standby`. `packages/envoy/cmd/listener/README.md` holds the run model, the readiness contract and the two-build rules.

The listener keeps the interest, session and CI buckets in memory through watcher-fed caches (`kvwatch`, shared by `store`, `session` and `cistore`); every pod runs its own. A start reads each bucket in full; a rewatch after a NATS reconnect or a self-health rebuild resumes after the last revision its cache applied, so it delivers only what was written since, and reads in full again for a recreated bucket, after a scan that did not complete, or when its resuming watcher cannot be created (logged at WARN). The session cache gives each entry a deadline one TTL after its write, since JetStream delivers no event when the bucket's `MaxAge` expires a key: reads report an entry past its deadline as absent and change nothing, and the entry leaves the cache at that deadline on a timer.

The interest reaper, run by the lease holder alone, deletes a dead session's interest from the `envoy_interests` bucket only 15 minutes after the row's last write: the grace window it is given, `store.InterestReapGrace` (10 minutes), plus `store.InterestRefreshAge` (5 minutes), the most a row's last write may lag the session's last heartbeat.
