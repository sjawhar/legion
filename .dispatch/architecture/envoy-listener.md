Ingests GitHub, Slack and Ghost Wispr webhooks, publishes ordinary notifications through JetStream, and role lanes and ad-hoc `notifications.` topics the stream does not capture through core NATS, resolves live sessions; keeps a durable consumer per session (`s-<session id>`) and relays it in stream order, as core frames on `notifications.envoy.deliver.<session id>` acknowledged on the session's `done`; forwards each role message once from one shared role-lane queue group; tracks PR check settlement. Its publish route answers a role or ad-hoc topic message once each recipient confirmed it (the role's holder, or each self-subscribed session registered on the topic), or 504 naming who did not. The CI store's summary loop is event-driven: the store's cache marks each record that changes, the loop keeps the records waiting out their debounce, a claim or the handover grace in a queue ordered by deadline, and it runs a pass only on a one-second tick at which a record changed or fell due, so with nothing changed it does no work and a change costs the same at any cache size. The `ENVOY_NOTIFICATIONS` stream and each KV bucket hold the deployment's replica count (`ENVOY_NATS_REPLICAS`), and the stream has a byte limit (`ENVOY_STREAM_MAX_BYTES`) past which it refuses new publishes (`DiscardNew`, a retryable `bus.ErrStreamFull` the webhook answers 503) and keeps every stored message until its 72-hour age; a clustered server's duplicate acknowledgement with no sequence, for a retry of a refused message (`bus.ErrNotStored`), has the bus publish the message once more without its `Nats-Msg-Id`, which the stream stores once it has room, and is never a success. A start raises a stream or bucket found with fewer copies, and a stream with a smaller byte limit or none, never lowers either, and keeps the stream's metadata, `envoy_stream_id` included, and its other limits on every update. JetStream's stream update has no compare-and-swap, so a concurrent start with other or no settings can put back the copies, limit and metadata it read; every owning program (the listener, Dispatch's server) therefore runs a keeper that every 30 seconds raises the stream's copies and byte limit back to its settings, puts back the discard policy and `envoy_stream_id`, and raises its own buckets' copies. An operator's raise of the limit stands, and the stream holds the most copies and the largest limit any program of a deployment carries. The small packages that instrument that path belong to it rather than to components of their own: OpenID Connect token verification for service accounts and the authorization code flow Dispatch's sign-in runs (`oidc`), the signature and duplicate-delivery guards (`verify`, `dedupe`), `metrics`, `id`, `logging`, listener bootstrap (`config`), the `natstail` diagnostic tail, and the listener's own harnesses (`integration`, `smoke`, `testnats`). `internal/dispatch/config` sits under the Dispatch tree but belongs here: it loads the shared `envoy.json` shape — NATS URLs, the Dispatch server URL and token — that `natstail`, the Dispatch server and the Envoy plugin all read. A session's registry entry, and its row in `GET /v1/sessions` and `GET /v1/registry/<id>`, carry the `plugin` and `plugin_version` its registration sent; a session from a plugin older than those fields has neither key.

Beside its two bearers (`ENVOY_API_TOKEN` and a projected service-account token the `ENVOY_OIDC_*` verifier judges) `/v1` takes one credential that is not a bearer: an access token the secrets broker issued, presented under RFC 9449's DPoP scheme (`cmd/listener/brokerauth.go`, over `packages/envoy/internal/brokertoken`). Four variables turn it on, all or none — `ENVOY_AGENT_SECRETS_URL`, the broker's base URL; one of `ENVOY_AGENT_SECRETS_ACCOUNT_TOKEN_FILE` (a listener in a pod) or `ENVOY_AGENT_SECRETS_GOOGLE_AUDIENCE` (a listener on a machine, whose own Google identity token `internal/googleidentity` reads at call time), which is the account the broker finds in its `BROKER_API_ACCOUNTS`; and `ENVOY_PUBLIC_URL`, the origin a proof's `htu` is checked against. Spent proofs go in the `envoy_dpop_proofs` KV bucket, raised by the same keeper as the others, so a proof captured from one replica is refused by every other and a bus with no JetStream refuses the start rather than keeping them in one process's memory. The credential opens and closes nothing: it says who is calling, never whether `/v1` may be reached, and a caller the broker names is held to its own session on the eight routes that act as one. A listener with none of the four variables set takes no such credential and reads `Authorization: DPoP` as it read every Authorization header it did not know before, so the clients that present one to whatever listener they are pointed at are served exactly as they were. A token naming a service and no person — a machine nobody has signed in on — is admitted like any other.

The listener keeps the interest, session and CI buckets in memory through watcher-fed caches (`kvwatch`, shared by `store`, `session` and `cistore`). A start reads each bucket in full, and so does every rewatch after a NATS reconnect or a self-health rebuild (`kvwatch.Watcher.Rewatch`), which resumes from no revision: the copy keeps every row while that read runs, each delivered record replaces its row as it arrives, and only a completed read removes the rows it did not find, so an incomplete read removes nothing and the listener reads again itself (a failed rewatch is logged at WARN). The session cache gives each entry a deadline one `MaxAge` after its write, since JetStream delivers no event when the bucket's `MaxAge` expires a key: the entry leaves the cache at that deadline on a timer, and a read that misses it goes through to the bucket. That deadline bounds the cache alone and decides no session's state.

A session is `live`, `unresponsive` or `gone`, computed from its registration record's `UpdatedAt` and never from cache or bucket membership: within `ENVOY_SESSION_LIVE_WINDOW` (5m by default); past it but within `ENVOY_SESSION_GONE_HORIZON` (1h by default); or past the horizon, or with no record at all. An `unresponsive` session is not dead — it keeps its role claims and its issue claims, a message sent to it is stored for it, and nothing starts a replacement for it — so every list row, every lookup, every refusal that names a session and every role answer carries `state` and `last_seen`, a role answer the holder's `holder_state` and `claim_released`. No lookup, publish, role-lane delivery, Dispatch outbox forward or daemon probe releases a role claim, whatever the holder's state: the listener's routes and lane and Dispatch's outbox resolve a holder through one resolution (`internal/roles`) that writes nothing to the role bucket, and only the role-claim reaper takes a claim away, running every `ENVOY_ROLE_CLAIM_REAP_INTERVAL` (5m by default), a period of its own and not the session reaper's. A claim renewed inside the live window is kept whatever the session bucket says of the id its holder registered under, which is what keeps the role of a process whose session id a second process resumed and then deregistered. Past that window a pass weighs the holder's session exactly as it did before a claim named a process (dispatch://INFRA-392): an unmarked claim is kept while that session reads short of `gone` and freed once it is gone, for a claim that names a process and one that names none alike, so a holder that has stopped answering keeps its role. The one claim a pass takes from a session that still answers is one a hand-back marked (`handed_back`), and only once its own renewal has lapsed, since no registration renews a marked claim: that is how a role an older client handed back comes free one live window plus up to one reap interval later — about ten minutes with these defaults — while that client still runs. Dispatch judges a role route's holder by the same two windows, read from the same two variables, so a deployment gives its listeners and its Dispatch server the same values. A Legion role whose agent process exits is `gone` at once. `ENVOY_SESSION_MAX_AGE` sets the `envoy_sessions` bucket's `MaxAge` when it is set and leaves the bucket as it is when it is not; `/healthz` reports the age in use as `session_max_age`.

The listener's interest reaper deletes a gone session's interest from the `envoy_interests` bucket only 15 minutes after the row's last write: the grace window it is given, `store.InterestReapGrace` (10 minutes), plus `store.InterestRefreshAge` (5 minutes), the most a row's last write may lag the session's last heartbeat.

A session's registry entry also carries the `client_instance_id` its registration sent: one Oh My Pi Envoy extension instance, one Claude Code channel-server process, each minting that id when it starts. A claim and a release state that same id for themselves now, and a role claim record names the process holding it (`client_instance_id`) and the moment that process last answered for it (`renewed_at`), both optional, so a record an earlier build wrote carries neither and is read at its `claimed_at`. Every registration renews the claims the registering session holds that name the registering process, and the first registration of that session id stating any instance adopts a claim that names none — any process of the session, not only the one that took the role; a renewal that cannot run never fails the registration. It renews every unmarked claim it holds whatever topics it carries, and reads no topic list at all: what says a role was given up is the mark a hand-back leaves on the claim it could not free (`store.RoleClaim.HandedBack`), no registration renews a marked claim, and the role claim reaper frees that role at its first pass past the live window. A registration that states no process renews the claims of its own session that name none, leaving them naming none, and adopts none, so no claim standing at a deploy ages out for want of a heartbeat. A soft claim on `POST /v1/roles/set` takes the role from a holder that is not `live`, and from the predecessor it names (`previous_session_id`) when that predecessor is not `live` either, when its live entry carries the claimant's own instance id, or when either entry carries no instance id, which the listener logs with both session ids. Where the role is already held under the claimant's own session id the route decides on the standing claim and not on the session record: granted where the claim names this very process, granted where nobody renewed the claim inside the live window and the role claim view can answer, and otherwise 409 naming that session, so a second process that resumed a live session's id cannot take the role that session holds. The hand-back mark is read nowhere in that decision, and every grant writes a fresh record, so a grant to the process a marked claim names clears the mark. That renewal arm is gated on the view's own state (`store.Registry.RoleViewErr`) because nothing but a registration renews a claim and a registration renews through that very view: while the view cannot answer — the state a listener restart leaves until its first read of the bucket completes — every claim's renewal ages with nothing able to move it, and a refill longer than the live window would hand a live holder's role to the second process, so that one arm answers the bus-unavailable 503 with `Retry-After: 1` naming the view and claims nothing. The arm that grants to the process the standing claim names rests on no renewal and is not gated: it is that holder re-claiming its own role after the restart that left the view refilling. Any other live holder keeps the role, a live predecessor of another client instance among them, and the claim is a 409 naming it. The predecessor's entry is read from the `envoy_sessions` bucket itself rather than from the trailing cache, and the claimant's entry is read from the bucket too, falling back to the cached entry when that read does not answer, which is the one place the predecessor rule still reads a stored instance — where the request states none. `POST /v1/interests/unsubscribe` states the process handing the interests back and frees only the role claims naming it, freeing a claim that names no process outright and leaving every claim another process holds, while `DELETE /v1/interests/<session_id>` keeps its force and is the one caller that forces. It answers in `removed` the topics that left the record rather than the ones the request named, because a role lane topic whose claim this release did not free stays in the record: one interest record serves every process running under one session id, and that topic is the interest half of a claim that still stands, which the claim wrote there and no delivery rides on — a role lane never enters a session's consumer filter, and a role message goes to its holder's agent subject with the holder read off the claim. A release that states no process, does not force, and names a role's own lane topic is a hand-back and marks the claim it could not free, which is the shape a client built before a release named its process sends; a current client reads that mark as a role it does not hold and claims the role back at its next heartbeat. A release that loses its conditional delete to a record written since reads the claim again, and one still naming that same session and that same process is its own holder's renewal, so the delete goes out again at the revision that renewal left, three deletes in all; a claim renewed under every one of them is left standing and fails the route naming the role, rather than reporting a release it did not make. `DELETE /v1/sessions/<id>` takes the same id as the `client_instance_id` query parameter and then removes the entry only while it carries that id or none, answering `200 {"deleted":true}` or `200 {"deleted":false,"reason":…}`.

`GET /v1/roles/<role>` reports `claim_revision` on every holder answer, the `envoy_roles` revision of the role's claim record, which stands whatever state its holder is in, and `0` for a role no record claims. It reports `client_instance_id` beside the holder, the process the holder's claim names, on the 200 and on a lapsed holder's 404 alike, and leaves the field out of both where the claim names no process — one an earlier build wrote, or one whose claimant stated none: a claimant refused the role held under its own session id reads an absent field as no other process holding it and an empty one as another process holding it, so a 200 that wrote an empty instance would send such a claim's own holder a soft claim it does not need and a 409 naming itself. It reports `handed_back` on both answers on the same terms, `true` where the claim carries the hand-back mark and the key left out where it carries none, so a client sees that the holder gave the role back and that no registration renews the claim any more; both clients read a marked claim as a role they do not hold (`roleHeldBy` in `@legion/envoy-client/transport`), so a holder marked by an older client's hand-back claims the role again at its next heartbeat and has it back. `POST /v1/roles/set` takes the revision back as `if_claim_revision` and then lands the claim only while the bucket still holds that revision: any other revision, a record where the claim names none, or none where it names one is a 409 carrying the revision the bucket does hold, and so is a claim that lost its conditional write to a claim that landed in between. No claim record is written, so the role keeps the holder the standing claim gave it. That is the one claim a scheduler can make conditional on the read it decided from, and the one that is never decided again, the revision it names being the caller's own precondition. A hard claim with no `if_claim_revision` is weighed by the last-claim-wins rule alone, while a soft one with none carries a precondition the route supplies in the caller's place: the revision of the claim record the route decided on (`store.RoleClaim.Revision`, `0` where no record stands), because the route decides on one read of that record and `store.Registry.SetRoleWithPrevious` writes against a second read of its own, and without it two processes of one session id claiming one unheld role in the same moment were both granted it. A record replaced under such a claim is no answer for a caller that asked nothing about revisions: the route reads the claim again, makes the whole decision again on what the second read finds, and tries once more, a second mismatch answered with the same 409.
